FINMA · Enforcement · 29 September 2026

Russian PEP enforcement: risk appetite must survive commercial pressure

What changed: FINMA concluded a bank-specific enforcement proceeding involving private-debt lending and client relationships linked to two Russian PEPs. It found serious deficiencies in risk management and AML compliance, including weak source-of-wealth scrutiny, insufficient challenge of negative media and suspicious conduct, an unjustified KYC exception, conflicts involving an employee and external intermediaries, and AMLA reporting breaches. The ruling is not yet legally binding. [S1]

No new general rule. The measures ordered apply to Julius Bär. For another IAM, the publication is a supervisory signal and a factual test of existing AML, governance and risk-appetite controls. The review below is conditional and proportionate to the IAM's Russian-client and introducer exposure.

Conditional action plan

Applicability
AML PEP Risk appetite Introducers
The IAM has stated that it has Russian clients and a business introducer but no PEP clients. The review is relevant to confirm that PEP and PEP-linked status, source of wealth, negative media, exceptions and introducer conflicts remain correctly controlled.
Responsible person / function
Primary execution: designated AML Officer and outsourced compliance/AML provider
Client and transaction facts: Portfolio Management. Independent challenge and risk-appetite aggregation: in-house Risk Manager. Legal analysis and exceptions: in-house Legal Counsel. Approval and oversight of risk appetite and material exceptions: Directors.
Due / trigger
No external deadline for another IAM. Review timing: To be set internally. Immediate escalation remains required whenever the existing AMLA suspicion or reporting threshold is met.
Status
Targeted control review
  • Confirm current PEP screening for clients, beneficial owners, controlling persons, authorised persons and material connected persons; do not equate “not a PEP” with “not linked to a PEP”.
  • For higher-risk relationships, test whether the source of wealth and source of funds are supported by evidence and reconciled with the client's activity, structure, transactions and public information.
  • Check whether negative media and unusual conduct are assessed critically, resolved in writing and escalated where the explanation does not dispel the concern.
  • List every KYC or risk-policy exception, the approving person, independent challenge, expiry and later review. A relationship-manager endorsement is not a substitute for objective evidence.
  • Check introducer and employee remuneration, family or personal connections and other incentives that could weaken onboarding, periodic review, transaction escalation or exit decisions.
  • Compare the Russian-client population and related revenue, countries, structures, custodians, flows and instruments with the approved AML and reputational risk appetite; escalate any concentration or relationship that falls outside it.
  • Confirm that additional clarification and MROS decision-making cannot be overridden by revenue, seniority, personal endorsement or introducer influence.
Evidence
PEP and connected-person screening record; refreshed source-of-wealth files where risk requires it; negative-media and unusual-activity assessments; exception register; introducer and employee conflict assessment; risk-appetite comparison; AML escalation or MROS decision record where applicable; independent Risk review; and Directors' oversight record.

Findings that matter beyond the bank

  • FINMA states that the affected clients linked to two Russian PEPs were high risk and that the bank did not adequately verify and scrutinise the origin of their assets over several years. [S1]
  • The bank did not critically assess negative media and suspicious client behaviour and breached AMLA reporting obligations. [S1]
  • A 2019 KYC exception was supported by an employee endorsement and was not questioned later despite the employee's close personal ties to the PEP family. [S1]
  • FINMA also described conflicts and misguided incentives, including millions in salary and commission earned by employees and external intermediaries. [S1]
  • The bank later redefined its risk appetite, started exiting incompatible clients, strengthened controls, revised remuneration and governance and undertook cultural reform. These were bank-specific remediation measures, not a prescribed checklist for every IAM. [S1]
