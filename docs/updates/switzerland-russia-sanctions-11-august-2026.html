Swiss sanctions · Binding Swiss measure

Switzerland adds 54 Russia-related sanctions targets

What changed: the EAER amended Annexes 2 and 8 of the Ukraine Ordinance on 10 August 2026. The official delta adds nine individuals and 45 entities to Annex 8 and amends 11 existing records across Annexes 8 and 2. It took effect on 11 August 2026 at 23:00. [S1] [S2, pp. 1–23]

Action plan

Applicability
Sanctions
Action required. The new Annex 8 entries are subject to the Swiss freezing and non-availability rules. Article 15 covers funds and economic resources owned or controlled by listed persons and entities, persons acting on their behalf or direction, and owned or controlled entities. Article 16 requires relevant assets or relationships to be reported to SECO without delay. [S4, Articles 15–16]
Responsible person / function
Proposed internal allocation based on the IAM's current structure:
Primary screening and match review: outsourced compliance/AML provider
Internal control and escalation: designated AML Officer. Portfolio and issuer exposure: in-house Risk Manager and Portfolio Management. Ownership/control and legal-nexus analysis: in-house Legal Counsel. Freezing, reporting and material-case oversight: Directors.
Due / trigger
Legally effective: 11 August 2026 at 23:00. A confirmed in-scope asset must be frozen and must not be made available; an Article 16 notification to SECO is due without delay. The law sets no later date for completing an IAM-wide control rescreen. Internal full-book rescreen target: To be set internally. That internal target does not postpone the binding measures when a match is identified. [S2, p. 1] [S4, Articles 15–16]
Status
Open · effective
  • Update the operative control source. Confirm that the screening vendor or manual process uses the SECO consolidated list dated 11 August 2026 or a later official version. [S2] [S3]
  • Re-screen the relevant exposure population. As a proposed control response to Articles 15–16, include clients, beneficial owners and controllers, authorised persons, payment and transaction parties, portfolio issuers and securities, banks, custodians and other counterparties through which funds or economic resources may be owned, controlled or made available. The precise population depends on the IAM's actual relationships and assets. [S4, Articles 15–16]
  • Resolve ownership and control, not only names. For a potential hit, use the identifiers in the official delta and analyse whether Article 15(1)(b) or (c) extends the restriction to a person acting on behalf or direction or to an owned or controlled entity. [S2] [S4, Article 15(1)]
  • Apply the binding measures to confirmed hits. Freeze affected funds or economic resources, do not make them available directly or indirectly, and notify SECO without delay with the information required by Article 16. [S4, Articles 15–16]
  • Run the AMLA analysis separately. FINMA states that notification to SECO does not replace additional clarification under Article 6 AMLA. If suspicion cannot be dispelled, an immediate report to MROS under Article 9 AMLA may be required. [S1] [S5, Articles 6 and 9]
  • Check the Annex 2 amendments only where the activity creates the relevant nexus. The five Annex 2 records are amended restricted-end-user data, not new Annex 8 asset-freeze additions. Their relevance depends on involvement in the goods, financing or support activity governed by the applicable export-control provisions. [S2, pp. 21–23]
Evidence
The official measures do not prescribe a special full-book rescreen file. A proposed internal record should retain the list version and screening date, population screened, results, match-resolution and ownership/control analysis, restrictions applied, and any SECO or MROS submission or decision.

What the official delta establishes

  • Annex 8 additions: nine individuals and 45 entities — 54 new targets in total — were added under the financial-sanctions and entry/transit provisions. [S2, pp. 1–19]
  • Eleven amendments: the delta contains six amended Annex 8 records (five individuals and one entity) and five amended Annex 2 entity records. This 6/5 split is derived directly from the two amendment sections in the delta; the FINMA notice reports the combined total of 11. [S1] [S2, pp. 19–23]
  • Source of listings: the delta identifies the origin as the European Union; the entries became binding in Switzerland through the Swiss amendment. [S2]

Effective-date clarification

The official delta expressly states entry into force on 11 August 2026. FINMA's page was published on 12 August and uses “today” at 23:00, creating an apparent one-day inconsistency. This brief follows the operative delta date and treats the amendment as effective from 11 August 2026 at 23:00. [S1] [S2, p. 1]
