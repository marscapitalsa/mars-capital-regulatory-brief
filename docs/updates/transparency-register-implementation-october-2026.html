Switzerland · Transparency · Effective 1 October 2026

Transparency Register: prepare the IAM's beneficial-owner filing

What changed: the Act on the Transparency of Legal Persons and the Identification of Beneficial Owners and its ordinance enter into force on 1 October. A Swiss AG must identify, verify, retain and file the prescribed information on its beneficial owners, subject to the applicable transition rule. [S1] [S2]

Two separate workstreams. The IAM must first comply as a Swiss legal entity filing its own beneficial-owner information. Separately, as a financial intermediary, it will later use register information within AML due diligence and address qualifying discrepancies. These roles have different triggers and should not be merged into a single generic “register review”.

Due / trigger
First Commercial Register change after 1 October: filing within one month. Without that trigger, an AG subject to ordinary audit: 1 January 2027; an AG under limited audit or opting out: 1 March 2027. Confirm the audit category before selecting the date.
Status
Preparation required
  • Confirm legal form, audit category and whether every UBO is already entered in the Commercial Register in a relevant capacity.
  • Map direct and indirect capital, voting and other control rights through each level of the ownership chain.
  • Identify the natural person or persons meeting the 25% capital or voting threshold or exercising control by other means; if none can be identified, apply the senior-governing-body rule.
  • Collect and verify identity, domicile and residence-country information and retain the supporting documents.
  • Record the applicable control bracket and the required information on intermediate entities or fiduciary arrangements.
  • Create or confirm AGOV/EasyGov access and filing authority; determine whether an internal person or authorised provider submits the filing.
  • Implement a one-month update process for later changes and a corresponding shareholder or controller notification workflow.
  • Keep the client-side register consultation and discrepancy process distinct from the IAM's own filing.
Evidence
Signed ownership-and-control chart; UBO determination; verification documents; audit-category confirmation; deadline determination; AGOV/EasyGov authority; submitted filing and receipt; change-monitoring procedure; and Directors' approval or oversight record.

Who is treated as the beneficial owner?

The SAM / Grant Thornton guide summarises the test as the natural person who ultimately holds at least 25% of capital or voting rights or controls the entity by other means. Indirect ownership is traced through intermediate entities; where no natural person can be determined, the most senior member of the governing body is recorded. The register also requires a control bracket and specified information on the control chain. [S3]

Supporting documents are generally retained by the entity rather than attached automatically to the filing. The guide states a ten-year retention period. Access to the register is restricted rather than public and is available to specified authorities, supervisory bodies and financial intermediaries or advisers for due-diligence purposes.

Determine the first filing date

  • Early trigger: the first Commercial Register change after 1 October 2026 requires the transparency filing within one month, even if the longer transition period would otherwise apply.
  • AG subject to ordinary audit: the guide gives 1 January 2027 as the backstop.
  • AG subject to limited audit or opting out: the guide gives 1 March 2027.
  • Special long transition: where all UBOs are already entered in the Commercial Register in the relevant capacity, the guide identifies a maximum transition extending to 1 October 2028. The precise conditions must be checked before relying on it.

Because the IAM's audit category has not been established in the reviewed materials, the correct backstop cannot be selected by assumption.

Client-side use is a later and different obligation

  • The legislation does not impose a general requirement to consult the register for every client relationship. The open ASG / FBT analysis explains that consultation may nevertheless be required by the general risk-based duty of diligence in particular circumstances. [S5]
  • The guide recommends incorporating register consultation into onboarding and periodic review from Q3 2027. This timing is a professional recommendation, not a statutory commencement date.
  • Statutory discrepancy reporting begins on 1 April 2027. The FBT analysis states that a report is required where a discrepancy capable of calling the BO information into doubt persists after the client has been contacted and given a reasonable period to resolve it. [S5]
  • Where reporting is required, the FBT analysis gives a 30-day period running from expiry of the remediation period or receipt of a response that does not resolve the discrepancy. [S5]
