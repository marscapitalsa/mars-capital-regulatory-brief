EU sanctions · Foreign-regime update

EU lists five executives supporting Russia's military-industrial complex

What changed: on 7 August 2026 the Council listed five senior figures at Russian defence and military-technology companies. The measures include an asset freeze, a prohibition on making funds or economic resources available directly or indirectly to the listed persons or for their benefit, and an EU travel ban. [S1] [S2]

Status
Conditional review
  • Determine whether the relevant relationship or asset has an EU legal or provider nexus.
  • If that condition applies, screen all five persons using the identifying data and reasons in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/1940, rather than relying only on the names highlighted in the press release. [S2]
  • Where there is a potential match, assess whether funds or economic resources would be made available directly or indirectly or for the listed person's benefit under the applicable EU rules.
  • Continue to check SECO before treating the EU listing as a Swiss-law measure. [S4]
Evidence
No general evidence requirement for a Swiss IAM arises from the EU listings alone. If an EU or contractual nexus is confirmed, retain the nexus conclusion and screening or escalation record required by the applicable procedure.

What the official publications establish

  • The Council describes all five persons as holding senior positions in Russian defence or military-technology companies involved in developing, producing or supplying equipment used by the Russian armed forces. [S1]
  • The press release specifically identifies Ramil Nailevich Badgutdinov of JSC Serpukhov Plant Metallist and Aleksandr Yurevich Dyukarev of JSC Krasnoyarsk Machine-Building Plant. The complete five-person list and identifying particulars appear in the implementing regulation. [S1] [S2]
  • The legal basis is the EU territorial-integrity sanctions framework under Regulation (EU) No 269/2014 and Decision 2014/145/CFSP. [S2] [S3]

Swiss-law position

A separate Swiss list amendment took effect on 11 August 2026 at 23:00. Its official delta adds nine individuals and 45 entities and amends 11 existing records across Annexes 8 and 2; it does not identify the five persons in the EU act of 7 August as those additions. The EU five-person listing therefore remains a foreign-regime topic unless an EU, banking, custody or contractual nexus exists, while the separate Swiss delta requires the action covered in the August brief's Swiss-sanctions topic.
