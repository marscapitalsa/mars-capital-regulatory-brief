Switzerland / EU · 4–5 November 2026 · Sanctions

Withdrawing funds from newly listed banks: what is prohibited?

What changed: Switzerland extended transaction bans to 33 Russian banks and 17 financial and crypto firms, and separately listed Kyrgyzstan’s Eco-Islamic Bank. The listings took effect at 23:00 Swiss time on 4 November. From 5 November, SECO can authorise qualifying withdrawals and account closures. The EU already restricts transactions with these institutions under its July package and has a separate authorisation process and earlier application dates. [S1] [S2] [S3] [S4]

Own money is not an exemption. A person subject to the relevant rules generally cannot withdraw cash, instruct a transfer of their own balance, or move a balance as part of closing an account if doing so involves a prohibited transaction with a listed institution. A bank, custodian or IAM that executes or routes the withdrawal can be caught even if the money belongs to a different person. Check the exact ban, any applicable exception, and the terms of any required authorisation before execution. [S3] [S4] [S5]

What an EU person cannot do

Regulation 833/2014 applies to EU nationals even outside the EU and to entities constituted under a Member State’s law wherever they operate. It also applies within EU territory and to entities in respect of business done wholly or partly in the EU. The test is not whether the transfer is denominated in euros or whether the client is Russian. A Swiss client’s instruction does not remove an EU bank’s obligations on its own leg of the transaction. Conversely, assess the Swiss IAM’s own EU nexus separately. [S5, Art. 13]

  • Withdrawing personally: an EU national cannot assume that a cash withdrawal in Russia or a transfer to their own non-listed account is allowed. The listed bank is still a counterparty to the transaction. Inquiring about the rules or applying for a licence is distinct from giving an instruction to move the money. [S4] [S5, Arts. 5h, 5ac, 5ad and 13]
  • Moving money for someone else: an EU bank, custodian, investment manager or other in-scope participant must not execute, arrange or process the payment where that amounts to direct or indirect participation in a prohibited transaction. A client signing the instruction, an unlisted intermediary, a non-EU beneficiary, or a change of currency does not by itself cure the restriction. Knowingly and intentionally participating in circumvention is separately prohibited, including where the participant is aware of the possibility and accepts it. [S5, Arts. 5h, 5ac, 5ad and 12–13]
  • Receiving proceeds: distinguish an EU receiving or correspondent bank processing the withdrawal itself from someone later receiving money in a genuinely separate transaction. The latter is not prohibited under these articles merely because funds once passed through a listed bank. Check whether the recipient is indirectly participating in the same transaction or a circumvention arrangement, and whether another sanctions measure applies. [S5, Arts. 5h, 5ac, 5ad and 12]
  • Knowing about someone else’s withdrawal: the transaction bans do not impose a general duty to police third-party accounts or freeze them merely because a listed bank is involved. However, Article 6b separately requires persons within scope who acquire information that would facilitate implementation of the regulation to provide it to the competent authority where they are resident or located within two weeks and to cooperate with verification, subject to the article’s confidentiality protections. The two-week notice of an authorisation granted under the new derogations is a separate obligation of the Member State, not of the account holder. [S4, Art. 1(14), (15), (20)] [S5, Arts. 6b and 13]

Which EU provision? Article 5h(1) / Annex XIV covers the 33 banks and extends to Russia-established entities more than 50% directly or indirectly owned by a listed entity. Article 5ad(1) / Annex XLV covers the 17 finance and crypto listings in this brief. Article 5ac(2) / Annex XLIV covers Eco-Islamic Bank. The three articles have different exceptions. For example, Article 5h(1a)(b) excludes transactions made by Member State nationals resident in Russia since before 24 February 2022; Article 5ac(5)(h) has a separate exception for transactions made by Member State nationals resident in third countries. Neither is a general exemption for EU nationals, nor should an intermediary assume that the client’s exception covers its own involvement. The separate EU ban on providing financial messaging services to the listed institutions is not lifted by the withdrawal derogation. [S4, recital 26 and Annexes III, VII–VIII] [S5, Arts. 5h, 5ac and 5ad]

What Swiss law prohibits

The Swiss Ordinance prohibits direct and indirect participation in transactions with the 33 banks under Article 27(1) / Annex 14, the 17 firms under Article 24c(1) / Annex 15b, and Eco-Islamic Bank under Article 27a(2) / Annex 14a. Article 27(1) also covers entities in Russia in which an Annex 14 entity holds a direct or indirect stake above 50%; Article 24c(1) also covers specified agents and mirror or successor entities. A Swiss IAM should not instruct or route an affected payment unless it can identify an applicable exception or obtain the necessary authorisation. The ban does not itself require every account to be closed. [S2] [S3, Arts. 24c, 27 and 27a]

Check exceptions before treating a balance as blocked. Article 27(2)(b) excludes transactions made by Swiss or EEA nationals resident in Russia since before 24 February 2022. Article 27a(3) excludes receipt of payments due under contracts concluded before 26 February 2026; paragraph 5 contains other specific exceptions. These are not blanket permission to transfer existing account balances. A transaction-ban listing alone is not an asset freeze. A separate match under Swiss Article 15 / Annex 8 or EU Regulation 269/2014, Article 2 / Annex I, requires a separate assessment of freezing, non-availability and reporting obligations. An authorisation under one transaction ban does not release assets frozen under a different provision. [S3, Arts. 15–16, 27 and 27a] [S7, Arts. 2 and 8]

When a withdrawal or account closure can be authorised

Switzerland: apply to SECO

Articles 27(4)–(5), 24c(3)–(4) and 30cter(2)–(3) cover institutions added to the relevant Swiss annex on or after 4 November 2026. The account must be held or controlled by a qualifying individual: a Swiss or EEA national, or someone with a temporary or permanent Swiss or EEA residence permit. The transaction must be necessary to end the activity, contract or arrangement with the listed institution. The destination must be a Swiss- or EEA-law financial institution, or a third-country financial institution owned or controlled by one. Apply within three months of the institution’s Swiss listing. Any permission is discretionary and valid for at most three months. [S3]

EU: apply to the competent Member State authority

Articles 5h(3), 5ad(4) and 5ac(8) cover institutions added to the relevant EU annex on or after 24 July 2026. The account must be owned or held by an EU, EEA or Swiss national, or an individual with a temporary or permanent permit there. Merely controlling a corporate account does not satisfy that wording. The transaction must be strictly necessary to withdraw funds or close the account in order to end the dealings with the listed institution. Funds must go to an EU Member State-law credit or financial institution, or a third-country institution owned or controlled by one. A Swiss bank does not qualify solely because it is Swiss. Apply within three months of the applicable date in the EU annex. Any permission is discretionary and valid for at most three months. [S4, Art. 1(14), (15), (20)]

EU filing dates for these listings: the 33 banks in Annex XIV, Eco-Islamic Bank in Annex XLIV and six of the 17 entities in Annex XLV have an applicable date of 13 August 2026, giving a three-month filing date of 13 November 2026. The other 11 Annex XLV entries have an applicable date of 23 August 2026, giving a filing date of 23 November 2026. See the names below. These are dates to apply, not permission to transact until then. Confirm the filing procedure and any applicable time-limit adjustment with the competent authority. Switzerland’s November listings do not restart either EU window. [S4, Annexes III, VII–VIII] [S6, Art. 3]

Example: a French national based in Switzerland wants to transfer a Cifra Bank balance to an independent Swiss bank. EU Article 5h applies to that national abroad. The destination may satisfy the Swiss criteria, but it is not eligible under the specific EU withdrawal derogation solely by virtue of being Swiss. Where the Swiss IAM participates and both prohibitions apply, a SECO decision does not replace the EU legal basis for the EU participant, nor does an EU decision replace the Swiss legal basis. Determine what authorisation covers each participant, the receiving institution and the actual payment chain before issuing an instruction. [S3, Art. 27] [S4, Art. 1(20) and Annex III] [S5, Art. 13]

Application to the IAM

  1. Identify the exact listed legal entity and its role. Ask the bank or custodian to confirm the originating, correspondent and receiving institutions and whether it can process the proposed route. If the IAM executes under a discretionary mandate, assess its own participation; if the client executes, distinguish explaining the rules from arranging the payment. [S3] [S5, Arts. 5h, 5ac, 5ad and 13]
  2. Do not instruct or process a prohibited leg. If the client seeks to end the relationship, confirm account ownership or control, qualifying status, necessity, destination eligibility, and the deadline under each applicable rule. File with the relevant authority if using a derogation; execute only within the resulting permission and its conditions. [S3] [S4, Art. 1(14), (15), (20)]
  3. Separately check for a freeze or reporting trigger. If an EU participant acquires relevant information, assess Article 6b even if it declines to handle the payment. Do not treat a refusal to execute under a transaction ban as a finding that the client’s balance is frozen. [S3, Arts. 15–16] [S5, Art. 6b] [S7, Arts. 2 and 8]
Evidence
For an affected case: the matched listing and annex, payment-chain confirmation, the applicable exception or authorisation and its conditions, and the execution or reporting decision. These are proposed internal records for the review.
