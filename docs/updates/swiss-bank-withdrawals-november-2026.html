Swiss sanctions · 4–5 November 2026 · EU rules where applicable

Withdrawing funds from newly listed banks: what is prohibited?

What changed: Switzerland extended transaction bans to 33 Russian banks and 17 financial and crypto firms, and separately listed Kyrgyzstan’s Eco-Islamic Bank. The listings took effect at 23:00 Swiss time on 4 November. From 5 November, SECO can authorise qualifying withdrawals and account closures. The EU already restricts transactions with these institutions under its July package and has a separate authorisation process and earlier application dates. [S1] [S2] [S3] [S4]

For a Swiss IAM, start with Swiss law. The ban can apply to withdrawing one’s own balance and to an IAM or bank that moves funds for a client. All 33 banks, all 17 financial and crypto firms and Eco-Islamic Bank are also subject to EU transaction bans. Apply the EU rules separately where they cover the person or transaction; matching lists do not make the two regimes interchangeable. [S2] [S3] [S4] [S5, Art. 13]

Swiss sanctions: who is covered and what is prohibited

Swiss sanctions generally apply on a territorial basis. They apply to a Swiss IAM acting from Switzerland and are not confined to Swiss passport holders: a foreign national acting in Switzerland can also be in scope. Conversely, Swiss citizenship alone does not generally bring a person established abroad within Swiss sanctions; assess any Swiss connection to conduct abroad case by case. The citizenship or residence criteria for a SECO authorisation are a separate question. [S3] [S8, territorial scope]

Swiss Articles 27(1), 24c(1) and 27a(2) prohibit direct or indirect participation in transactions with the institutions listed in Annexes 14, 15b and 14a respectively. Article 27(1) also covers Russia-based entities in which an Annex 14 institution holds a direct or indirect stake above 50%. Article 24c(1) also covers specified agents and mirror or successor entities. [S2] [S3, Arts. 24c, 27 and 27a]

  • Own account: where Swiss law applies, a client cannot use the fact that the money is theirs to justify withdrawing cash, transferring the balance to another account in their own name, or arranging a payout on closing the account. An applicable exception or authorisation is needed if the proposed withdrawal is prohibited. Asking about the rules or applying to SECO is distinct from instructing the transfer. [S3, Arts. 24c, 27, 27a and 30cter]
  • Client account: a Swiss IAM or bank must not instruct, arrange or process a withdrawal where that would amount to direct or indirect participation in a prohibited transaction. It does not matter by itself that the client signs the instruction or that an unlisted bank appears in the chain. Check the role of every participant. [S3, Arts. 24c, 27 and 27a]
  • Other people’s transactions: merely knowing of a withdrawal is not the same as taking part in it. The transaction ban alone does not require a client’s balance to be frozen or the account to be closed. A separate match under Article 15 and Annex 8 can trigger a freeze, a ban on making assets available and reporting under Article 16. Some banks, including Energobank and Moskommertsbank, appear in both Annex 8 and Annex 14. Check both annexes; an account-exit authorisation under the transaction ban does not by itself lift a freeze. [S2, pp. 48–49, 102–104] [S3, Arts. 15–16, 24c, 27 and 27a]

Check the exact exception. For example, Article 27(2)(b) excludes transactions made by Swiss or EEA nationals resident in Russia since before 24 February 2022. Article 27a(3) excludes receipt of payments due under contracts concluded before 26 February 2026, and paragraph 5 provides other specific exceptions. These are not blanket permission to transfer existing account balances. [S3, Arts. 27 and 27a]

EU sanctions: the same institutions, a separate test

EU Regulation 833/2014 applies to EU nationals and EU-law entities even outside the EU. It also applies in EU territory and to entities in respect of business done wholly or partly in the EU. Swiss citizenship or the use of euros is not by itself an EU trigger. For example, an EU bank processing a Swiss client’s transfer has its own EU obligations; assess the Swiss IAM’s EU exposure separately rather than assuming the client and each intermediary are subject to the same rules. [S5, Art. 13] [S8, foreign sanctions]

Which EU provision? Article 5h(1) / Annex XIV covers the 33 banks and extends to Russia-established entities more than 50% directly or indirectly owned by a listed entity. Article 5ad(1) / Annex XLV covers the 17 finance and crypto listings in this brief. Article 5ac(2) / Annex XLIV covers Eco-Islamic Bank. The three articles have different exceptions. For example, Article 5h(1a)(b) excludes transactions made by Member State nationals resident in Russia since before 24 February 2022; Article 5ac(5)(h) has a separate exception for transactions made by Member State nationals resident in third countries. Neither is a general exemption for EU nationals, nor should an intermediary assume that the client’s exception covers its own involvement. The separate EU ban on providing financial messaging services to the listed institutions is not lifted by the withdrawal derogation. [S4, recital 26 and Annexes III, VII–VIII] [S5, Arts. 5h, 5ac and 5ad]

Participation and reporting. An in-scope EU participant must not conduct or take part in a prohibited withdrawal or knowingly and intentionally participate in circumvention. A receiving or correspondent bank processing the withdrawal itself is different from a person later receiving funds in a genuinely separate transaction; assess whether the latter participates indirectly and whether any other sanctions apply. Separately, Article 6b requires persons within scope to report information that would facilitate implementation to the competent authority where they are resident or located within two weeks of acquiring it and cooperate with verification, subject to the article’s confidentiality protections. The two-week notification of an authorisation granted is a different obligation of the Member State, not the account holder. Check any separate asset freeze or reporting obligation under Regulation 269/2014. [S4, Art. 1(14), (15), (20)] [S5, Arts. 5h, 5ac, 5ad, 6b and 12–13] [S7, Arts. 2 and 8]

When a withdrawal or account closure can be authorised

Switzerland: apply to SECO

Articles 27(4)–(5), 24c(3)–(4) and 30cter(2)–(3) cover institutions added to the relevant Swiss annex on or after 4 November 2026. The account must be held or controlled by a qualifying individual: a Swiss or EEA national, or someone with a temporary or permanent Swiss or EEA residence permit. The transaction must be necessary to end the activity, contract or arrangement with the listed institution. The destination must be a Swiss- or EEA-law financial institution, or a third-country financial institution owned or controlled by one. Apply within three months of the institution’s Swiss listing. Any permission is discretionary and valid for at most three months. [S3]

EU: apply to the competent Member State authority

Articles 5h(3), 5ad(4) and 5ac(8) cover institutions added to the relevant EU annex on or after 24 July 2026. The account must be owned or held by an EU, EEA or Swiss national, or an individual with a temporary or permanent permit there. Merely controlling a corporate account does not satisfy that wording. The transaction must be strictly necessary to withdraw funds or close the account in order to end the dealings with the listed institution. Funds must go to an EU Member State-law credit or financial institution, or a third-country institution owned or controlled by one. A Swiss bank does not qualify solely because it is Swiss. Apply within three months of the applicable date in the EU annex. Any permission is discretionary and valid for at most three months. [S4, Art. 1(14), (15), (20)]

EU filing dates for these listings: the 33 banks in Annex XIV, Eco-Islamic Bank in Annex XLIV and six of the 17 entities in Annex XLV have an applicable date of 13 August 2026, giving a three-month filing date of 13 November 2026. The other 11 Annex XLV entries have an applicable date of 23 August 2026, giving a filing date of 23 November 2026. See the names below. These are dates to apply, not permission to transact until then. Confirm the filing procedure and any applicable time-limit adjustment with the competent authority. Switzerland’s November listings do not restart either EU window. [S4, Annexes III, VII–VIII] [S6, Art. 3]

Example: a Swiss-resident client asks a Swiss IAM to transfer a Cifra Bank balance to a Swiss bank. Start with Swiss Article 27 and check whether SECO authorisation is needed. If the client is also an EU national or an EU bank is involved, assess EU Article 5h for each in-scope participant. The Swiss destination may meet the Swiss test but does not qualify under the EU withdrawal derogation solely by virtue of being Swiss. Neither authorisation replaces the other where both are required. [S3, Art. 27] [S4, Art. 1(20) and Annex III] [S5, Art. 13]

For an affected case: the matched listing and annex, payment-chain confirmation, the applicable exception or authorisation and its conditions, and the execution or reporting decision. These are proposed internal records for the review.
