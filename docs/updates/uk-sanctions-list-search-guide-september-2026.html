UK government guidance · 11 September 2026

UK explains how to search its sanctions list without missing results

What changed: the UK government published detailed instructions for exact, partial and fuzzy searches in the UK Sanctions List search tool. The guide warns that fuzzy search is off by default, exact phrases and early filters may create false negatives, and using the tool does not reduce the user's due-diligence obligations or potential liability. [S1, sections “How the search works” to “Using filters”]

Swiss IAM relevance. This is UK operational guidance, not a new Swiss legal obligation. It is relevant where a relationship or transaction has a UK nexus, a bank or custodian contractually applies UK sanctions, or the IAM uses the UK search tool to check the quality of outsourced screening.

  • Search the full list without filters first; apply country, thematic and other filters only afterwards. [S1, “Using filters to narrow your search”]
  • Run fuzzy search separately because it is disabled by default and is designed to identify close spellings and transliterations. [S1, “Widening your search with fuzzy search”]
  • Do not rely only on a quoted exact phrase: name order and data formatting can cause a false negative. [S1, “Search for exact phrases using quotation marks”]
  • When entering several terms, account for the tool's rule that partial matching applies only to the last term; separate searches may be needed. [S1, “Partial matches”]
  • Consider thematic regimes as well as country regimes because a national may be designated under a regime unrelated to nationality. [S1, “Using filters to narrow your search”]
  • Complete a separate ownership-and-control analysis: sanctions prohibitions can apply to an unlisted entity owned or controlled by a designated person. [S1, introductory guidance]
  • Do not treat a bookmarked search URL as fixed evidence; the guide says displayed results can change when the list is updated. [S1, “Search URLs”]
Evidence
Proposed internal evidence: the search date and time, inputs and variants used, whether fuzzy search and filters were applied, the result reviewed, ownership/control analysis where relevant, and the disposition of any alert. This evidence format is an internal control proposal rather than a UK requirement stated in the guide.

Key search mechanics

  • Exact results: multiple unquoted terms are searched independently, so a search for two words may return entries containing either word. [S1, “Exact matches”]
  • Partial results: a single fragment can match a longer word with the same initial character. With multiple terms, only the final term receives partial matching. [S1, “Partial matches”]
  • Fuzzy results: one- or two-character terms receive no fuzzy results; three- or four-character terms allow one differing character; longer terms allow up to two. [S1, “Widening your search with fuzzy search”]
  • Filters: filters can narrow by regime, designation date, source, type and sanctions imposed, but the guide says filtering too early can hide a match. [S1, “Using filters to narrow your search”]
  • Liability: reliance on the tool or its results does not limit criminal or civil liability and does not reduce the obligation to undertake due diligence. [S1, introductory guidance]
