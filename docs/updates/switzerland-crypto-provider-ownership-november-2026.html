Switzerland · 5 Nov · Potentially relevant

Switzerland widens the crypto-provider ownership restriction

What changed: The amended Swiss rule covers acquisition of Swiss- or EEA-established or constituted entities providing crypto-asset services, regardless of the type of crypto service. Article 20(2bis) also covers control and management roles; paragraph 3 sets out exceptions. [S1] [S2]

Relevance to a Swiss IAM. High if the IAM participates in an affected deal or governance change; otherwise conditional. The restriction concerns the provider and the relevant person, not all holdings in portfolios that include crypto exposure.

What changed on 5 November

The change to Article 20(2bis)(a) broadens the category of provider to entities that provide crypto-asset services. The test is whether the entity is established or constituted under Swiss or EEA law and provides such services. Article 20(2bis) prohibits allowing covered persons to acquire or control such an entity directly or indirectly or to hold a role in its governing bodies. [S1] [S2] [S3]

Who is in scope

The starting point is Russian nationals and natural persons residing in Russia. Article 20(3) excludes Swiss, EEA and UK nationals and natural persons holding a temporary or permanent residence permit in Switzerland, the EEA or the UK. Confirm the actual status; do not infer it from a mailing address or from the custody jurisdiction. [S3]

What not to infer

This ownership and governance rule is distinct from the professional crypto-service restrictions under Article 20(2) and the ban involving Russia-based providers under Article 20b. A fund investment giving exposure to Bitcoin is not, merely by that fact, an acquisition of a crypto-service provider. Test the legal entity and rights actually acquired. [S3]

Action plan

Applicability
Sanctions Crypto
Relevant if a mandate, vehicle or deal involves a covered acquirer, controller or appointee and a crypto-service business. This is not a general ban on owning Bitcoin.
Responsible person / function
Execution: the relevant investment or transaction owner. In-house Legal Counsel: assess the target, proposed rights and any exception. Designated AML Officer or outsourced compliance: review identity and ownership information. Risk Manager and Directors: oversight where material.
Due / trigger
Effective 5 November 2026. Assess before allowing an in-scope transaction or appointment; no separate internal deadline is prescribed.
Status
Applicability to be confirmed

Application to the IAM

  1. Identify the target’s establishment, governing law and activities, and whether the proposed structure confers direct or indirect acquisition, control or a governing role. [S3]
  2. Check the relevant individual’s nationality, residence and any Article 20(3) exception. Do not proceed if the transaction would permit prohibited acquisition, control or appointment. [S3]
Evidence
For an affected case: target activity and legal status, proposed ownership or governance rights, relevant status and exception analysis.
