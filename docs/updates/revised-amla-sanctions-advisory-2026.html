Switzerland · AMLA · Effective 1 October 2026

Revised AMLA: sanctions risk and specified advisory activities

What changed: the revised AMLA and related ordinances enter into force on 1 October 2026. The reform expressly brings prevention of breaches of Swiss Embargo Act measures into the AML framework and extends AML due diligence to specified professional advisory activity involving higher-risk transactions. [S1] [S2]

Immediate IAM application. This is not limited to a policy acknowledgement. The IAM must determine its sanctions exposure, translate the conclusion into proportionate organisational measures and test whether any service outside ordinary portfolio management falls within the new adviser perimeter. The detailed implementation steps below reflect the SAM / Grant Thornton practical guide; they are association and professional-firm guidance, not a separate source of law. [S2] [S3]

1. Sanctions become an express AML organisational issue

The revised framework links AMLA organisation to prevention of breaches of coercive measures under the Embargo Act. FINMA's related AMLO-FINMA consultation states that financial intermediaries must understand ownership and control structures and that the ordinance will describe measures to prevent Embargo Act breaches in greater detail. The consultation text is not the final ordinance and should not be treated as such. [S4]

The SAM / Grant Thornton guide recommends calibrating the control framework to exposure:

  • Lower exposure: a documented risk conclusion, onboarding and periodic checks for clients, UBOs and signatories, and a clear escalation rule.
  • Medium exposure: intensified screening, a formal match/freezing/SECO-reporting process and identification and monitoring of sanctions-risk relationships.
  • Higher exposure: frequent or daily screening, documented restrictions on flows, custodians and instruments, second-level controls and periodic management review.

These categories are the guide's implementation model, not classifications stated in AMLA. Factors identified by the guide include domiciliary entities or trusts in control chains, broad powers of attorney, custodians outside Switzerland or the EU, client or UBO links to sanctioned jurisdictions or sectors, targeted instruments and payment flows through higher-risk countries.

IAM-specific inference: the disclosed presence of Russian clients, cross-border arrangements, foreign funds and introducer relationships means that a low-exposure conclusion should not be assumed. The actual result depends on residence, activity, UBOs, flows, instruments, custodians and counterparties in each relationship.

2. Test activities outside portfolio management

The guide describes a four-stage analysis:

  1. Catalogue: is the service connected with a transaction listed by the revised AMLA, such as specified real-estate dealings, creation or administration of a non-operating entity, contributions or distributions involving it, a company transaction through such an entity, formation of an entity abroad, or domiciliation?
  2. Participation: does the IAM effectively further the specific transaction? A client-tailored recommendation that advances a transaction may qualify; a general abstract memorandum ordinarily should not.
  3. Exemption: does a statutory exemption apply? The guide identifies, among others, specified family, matrimonial, inheritance and gift matters; certain transactions below CHF 5 million settled through Swiss financial institutions; specified owner-occupied Swiss residential property; a board mandate for an operating company; and certain foundations created on death. The conditions must be tested transaction by transaction.
  4. Group relationship: is the service genuinely provided within a group while the group entity remains the contracting party?

Examples in the guide indicate that coordinating financing and notarial implementation after the parties intend to transact, implementing a specific foreign structure, or providing a registered office for more than six months may be covered. Merely preparing a general note without participation in a particular transaction is treated differently.

3. Consequences where the activity is covered

  • The IAM remains a financial intermediary; the adviser regime is treated as subsidiary rather than creating a second institutional status.
  • AMLA due diligence applies to the covered activity from 1 October, including ongoing mandates: identification, UBO or controlling-person work, purpose, origin of funds, sanctions and PEP checks, risk classification and ongoing monitoring as applicable.
  • The covered relationships should be separately identifiable in the AML control population.
  • The risk analysis, directives and control plan should describe the additional activity and the responsible functions.
  • The guide refers to an optional declaration under which all relevant activity can be placed under the same AML due-diligence and supervisory framework. The effect and timing should be confirmed with the supervisory organisation before relying on that option.

4. Points still requiring confirmation

  • The Federal Council fixed the statutory commencement date, but some detailed FINMA provisions reviewed in September remained at consultation stage. [S1] [S4]
  • Final supervisory-organisation rules and forms should be checked before submitting the notification.
  • Coverage of a particular service depends on the actual steps performed, not its contract label.
  • No general numerical limit for Russian-client relationships is created by this reform. Any internal appetite threshold must be justified by the IAM's own risk assessment and approved governance.
