FINMA Guidance 01/2026 · Supervisory expectation

Crypto in an individual mandate requires a custody and segregation assessment

What changed: FINMA Guidance 01/2026 states how Article 24(1) FinIO applies when an individually managed portfolio contains crypto-assets. The portfolio manager must use a permitted custodian and assess prudential supervision, technical capability and bankruptcy segregation; restricted legacy arrangements are subject to cumulative conditions and explicit written client consent. [S1] [S2, pp. 5–7]

Action plan

Applicability
Crypto custody Products
Conditional action. Relevant if any discretionary mandate holds or is intended to hold crypto-assets, or if the IAM sponsors or manages a foreign product investing in crypto and places that product in client portfolios. It does not require a project where the documented exposure inventory is zero. [S2, pp. 5–7]
Responsible person / function
Proposed internal allocation:
Primary exposure inventory and pre-trade control: Portfolio Management and in-house Risk Manager
Custodian and legal classification: in-house Legal Counsel. Regulatory-control review: designated AML Officer with the outsourced compliance/AML provider. Approval of any exception, legacy arrangement or policy change: Directors.
Due / trigger
FINMA published the guidance on 12 January 2026 and states a current supervisory practice; it provides no separate transition deadline. Do not add crypto to a mandate or place a relevant foreign product before the custody analysis is complete. Review date for any existing exposure: To be set internally. [S1] [S2]
Status
Confirm exposure
  • Inventory direct and indirect crypto exposure. Check discretionary mandates and any foreign fund or product sponsored or managed by the IAM and placed in client portfolios. Record a no-exposure conclusion if none exists. [S2, pp. 5 and 7]
  • Classify the custodian. The standard Article 24(1) FinIO routes are a Swiss bank, securities firm or DLT trading facility, or a foreign institution subject to equivalent prudential supervision. For crypto, FINMA also expects appropriate technical infrastructure and expertise and legal bankruptcy segregation of client assets. [S2, pp. 5–6] [S3, Article 24(1)]
  • For foreign custody, document both supervision and insolvency treatment. Confirm the supervisory status and obtain a legal assessment that the foreign law provides protection equivalent to segregation in bankruptcy. [S2, pp. 5–6]
  • Do not treat a product wrapper as a workaround. FINMA states that a foreign fund structure cannot circumvent these principles when the IAM sponsors or manages the product and places it in individually managed portfolios. [S2, p. 7]
  • Use a legacy exception only if every stated condition is met. Guidance 01/2026 identifies limited cases involving either an equivalently supervised foreign custodian without equivalent bankruptcy protection or a Swiss SRO-supervised custodian with Article 242a DEBA segregation but without prudential supervision. The client must be comprehensively informed of the risks and suitable alternatives and must provide explicit written consent. [S2, pp. 6–7]
  • Connect the custody review to the August sanctions control. Where a mandate touches crypto, separately screen the asset, platform, provider, wallet, bank and execution route under Articles 20a and 20b of the Ukraine Ordinance. Custody suitability and sanctions permissibility answer different questions and both must be documented. [S4, Articles 20a–20b and Annex 13a]
  • Correct any non-compliant arrangement. FINMA states that institutions must adjust custody arrangements that do not comply with the described requirements. [S2, p. 7]
Evidence
Exposure inventory; mandate and product map; custodian licence and supervisory-status evidence; technical-capability assessment; bankruptcy-segregation legal analysis; client risk information and alternatives; explicit written consent where a legacy case is relied on; remediation record; and the separate Article 20a/20b sanctions assessment.

Decision test

  • No crypto exposure: retain the documented inventory conclusion and reassess before the first crypto purchase or relevant product placement.
  • Compliant standard custodian: document prudential supervision, technical suitability and bankruptcy segregation before use. [S2, pp. 5–6]
  • Legacy arrangement: all exception conditions, disclosure of risks and alternatives, and explicit written client consent must be present. [S2, pp. 6–7]
  • Unclear or non-compliant custody: do not add exposure and remediate the arrangement. [S2, p. 7]

Practical conclusion for the IAM

The first step is an exposure inventory, not a general crypto-policy project. If no mandate or IAM-sponsored or managed foreign product touches crypto, the topic can close until the trigger changes. If exposure exists or is planned, the custody file must establish the custodian's permitted status, technical capability and insolvency segregation before the asset is used, and the separate August sanctions screen must cover the asset and the execution chain.
