EU AI Act: what applies now and when it can reach a Swiss IAM

What changed: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 was published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and entered into force on 27 July. The AI Act's general application date remained 2 August 2026, but the Omnibus moved the core requirements for Annex III high-risk systems to 2 December 2027 and for high-risk systems embedded in Annex I products to 2 August 2028. Transparency rules remain relevant now, while new prohibitions and a legacy-system marking deadline apply from 2 December 2026. [S1] [S2, Article 1(39)–(40) and Article 4]

Status
Conditional applicability review
  • Identify AI systems used for EU-facing client interaction, public communications, HR, creditworthiness, biometric or emotion-recognition functions, and record where their output is used. This is a practical application step, not an inventory expressly required from every Swiss firm. [S3, Articles 2 and 50; Annex III]
  • For each potentially in-scope use, distinguish the IAM's role as provider or deployer. Using a third-party tool does not by itself make the IAM the provider of the underlying general-purpose model. [S3, Articles 2–3]
  • If an EU-facing AI system interacts directly with natural persons, check the Article 50(1) notice. For deepfakes or public-interest text, check the deployer disclosures in Article 50(4); the public-interest-text disclosure does not apply where there is human review or editorial control and a person assumes editorial responsibility. [S3, Article 50(1), (4)–(5)]
  • Do not classify ordinary drafting, research or portfolio analysis as high-risk merely because AI is used. High-risk treatment depends on Article 6 and Annex III or on integration into a regulated Annex I product. [S2, Article 1(8), (40)–(41)] [S3, Article 6 and Annexes I and III]
Evidence
The Act does not impose a universal standalone AI inventory on every Swiss user. If an in-scope use is identified, retain the applicability conclusion, the identified provider/deployer role and the notice, disclosure, editorial-control or provider documentation used to meet the specific applicable rule.

What applies and what was postponed

  • Scope: a third-country provider or deployer is covered where the AI output is used in the EU; providers placing systems or general-purpose models on the EU market are covered regardless of establishment. [S3, Article 2(1)(a)–(c)]
  • Transparency: providers of systems generating synthetic audio, image, video or text must use machine-readable marking under Article 50(2). Deployers have separate disclosure duties for deepfakes and certain public-interest text. [S3, Article 50(2), (4)]
  • Legacy provider grace period: providers of relevant systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 to comply with Article 50(2). This provision is addressed to providers, not automatically to every customer using an AI tool. [S2, Article 1(39)(b)]
  • New prohibited practices: the Omnibus adds targeted prohibitions concerning AI systems used to generate or manipulate non-consensual intimate material or child sexual abuse material; these provisions apply from 2 December 2026. [S2, Article 1(7), (40)(a)]
  • High-risk dates: the principal Chapter III requirements move to 2 December 2027 for Annex III systems and 2 August 2028 for high-risk systems embedded in Annex I regulated products. [S2, Article 1(40)(b)]

Likely relevance to the IAM

The currently known facts do not establish that the IAM supplies an AI system into the EU or uses AI output in the EU. Internal use of an off-the-shelf drafting or research tool in Switzerland is therefore not enough, on its own, to conclude that the Act applies. The issue becomes more material if AI is used in an EU-facing chatbot, public communications, employment decisions, natural-person creditworthiness assessments or another Annex III use case, or if the IAM materially modifies and supplies a system under its own name. This is a company-specific inference from the scope and classification provisions. [S3, Articles 2–3, 6 and Annex III]
