Swiss sanctions · Binding prohibitions and protective measures

Switzerland introduces new Russia-related crypto and sectoral rules

What changed: Switzerland implemented the remaining measures of the EU's 20th Russia sanctions package. Most provisions took effect on 20 August 2026. The amendment expressly adds uncertificated securities and crypto-assets to the definition of “funds”; prohibits transactions involving A7A5, RUBx and the digital rouble; and restricts transactions with Russian-established crypto-service providers and exchange or transfer platforms. It also adds activity-specific restrictions, transition provisions and defensive remedies for Swiss persons facing specified Russian measures. [S1] [S2, pp. 1, 8–18]

Action plan

Applicability
Sanctions
Action required. Article 20b(1) applies to the IAM as a Swiss legal person, expressly including a financial institution, and covers both direct and indirect transactions with Russian-established crypto-service providers and exchange or transfer platforms. Article 20a separately covers direct or indirect participation in transactions involving Annex 13a assets and support for their development. The remaining provisions apply only where their specific activity, contract, client or dispute nexus exists. [S2, Articles 20a–20b and Annex 13a, pp. 9 and 18]

Status
Implementation review required
  • Hold and classify any new or pending crypto-related transaction before execution. Check whether the instrument or route involves A7A5, RUBx, the digital rouble, a Russian-established crypto-service provider, or a Russian-established exchange or transfer platform. Article 20b is categorical and does not require the provider or platform to be separately named in an annex. [S2, Articles 20a–20b and Annex 13a, pp. 9 and 18]
  • Apply the correct actor test. In discretionary management, where the IAM places or instructs the transaction, the IAM acts as a legal person and the direct-and-indirect standard in Article 20b(1) applies. In an advisory-only relationship, identify who legally executes: Article 20b(2) captures only a natural person's direct transactions, while a client that is itself a legal person remains subject to Article 20b(1). This mandate-level treatment is the IAM-specific application of the different statutory wording and should be confirmed by Legal Counsel for the actual execution model. [S2, Article 20b(1)–(2), p. 9]
  • Request written exposure confirmation from the execution chain. As a proposed internal control, ask each relevant custodian, broker, bank, wallet provider and execution venue to confirm whether the contemplated service or route involves an Article 20a asset or an Article 20b Russian provider or platform. Use the response to classify the transaction; do not treat a service provider's confirmation as replacing the IAM's own legal assessment. [S2, Articles 20a–20b]
  • Include crypto in the asset-freeze control. The amended definition of “funds” expressly includes uncertificated securities and crypto-assets. A listed-person ownership or control match can therefore engage the Article 15 freezing and non-availability rules for crypto-assets as well as conventional financial assets. [S2, Article 1(a), p. 1; Article 15, p. 8] [S4, Articles 15–16]
  • Apply the sanctions and AMLA reporting chain. For a relationship or asset believed to fall under Article 15, Article 16 requires notification to SECO without delay. FINMA additionally states that a SECO report does not replace the additional clarifications required by Article 6 AMLA; if suspicion cannot be dispelled, an immediate report to MROS under Article 9 AMLA may be required. [S3] [S4, Articles 15–16] [S5, Articles 6 and 9]
  • Use Article 30csepties only as an authorisation route. SECO may authorise an exception to Article 20b where required to withdraw investments from Russia or terminate business activity there. It is not an automatic wind-down exemption. [S2, Article 30csepties, p. 14]
  • Map the transition provisions before relying on legacy contracts. Article 27(2)(a)(5) permits receipt of payments due from Annex 15 entities under contracts and obligations performed before 20 August 2026; it does not state a later cut-off. Articles 15(9undecies), 35(11) and 36(c)–(d) contain the separate 21 November 2026 and 25 January 2027 cut-offs described above. Record the exact article relied on for any continuing or wind-down transaction. [S2, Articles 15(9undecies), 27(2)(a)(5), 35(11) and 36(c)–(d), pp. 8–10 and 16–17]
  • Check the client-travel rule only for the narrow covered population. Article 29a(3) does not apply to Russian clients generally. It requires a copy of the prior notification to be carried only by Russian diplomatic or consular staff, specified mission personnel and covered household family members who are already subject to Article 29a(1), and produced on request while in Switzerland. [S2, Article 29a(3), p. 13] [S4, Article 29a(1)–(3)]
  • Preserve the defensive remedies where a Russian dispute exists. Article 29d(1bis)–(1ter) permits qualifying Swiss persons to seek a declaration of an agreed arbitral tribunal's exclusive jurisdiction and an order restraining specified foreign proceedings. Article 30f creates damages claims, including legal costs, in the circumstances stated there, including specified effects of Decree No. 302 and Federal Law 470-FZ. These are conditional legal remedies, not routine operational controls. [S2, Articles 29d and 30f, pp. 13 and 15–16]
  • Check the other sectoral measures only against actual exposure. Review maritime, LNG, goods, financing, managed-security-service, Russian-government-support, intellectual-property and Russian-judgment provisions only if the IAM's holdings, services, contracts or counterparties create the statutory nexus. Do not convert them into blanket portfolio restrictions. [S1] [S2, Articles 12c, 12e, 12h, 14h, 28e and 28g–28k]
Evidence
The Ordinance does not prescribe a special IAM review file. A proposed internal record should retain the mandate classification; instruments, platforms and transaction chain checked; written responses from custodians, brokers and execution venues; the ordinance and list versions used; any ownership/control or Article 20a/20b analysis; the transition or authorisation provision relied on; restrictions applied; and any SECO or MROS submission and director approval.

What matters most for the IAM

  • Crypto is now explicit in both categorical prohibitions and the asset-freeze framework. Articles 20a–20b regulate specified crypto transactions and Russian platforms, while Article 1(a) expressly treats crypto-assets as funds for the Ordinance. [S2, Articles 1(a), 20a–20b and Annex 13a]
  • Legal and natural persons are treated differently. Article 20b(1) captures direct and indirect transactions by legal persons; Article 20b(2) captures direct transactions by natural persons. [S2, Article 20b(1)–(2)]
  • Immediate reporting is not an internal deadline. Where Article 16 is engaged, notification to SECO is required without delay, and FINMA requires the separate AMLA clarification and MROS analysis. [S3] [S4, Article 16]
  • The package contains relief and protection as well as restrictions. It includes contract and divestment routes and new Swiss judicial remedies, each subject to its own conditions. [S2, Articles 27(2), 29d, 30csepties, 30f, 35 and 36]

Practical conclusion for the IAM

The first decision is whether any discretionary or advisory mandate, managed asset or execution chain touches the three named assets or a Russian-established crypto provider or platform. If the documented answer is no, the principal Article 20a/20b review can close, while the remaining package stays conditional on actual exposure. If the answer is yes or uncertain, hold the transaction, obtain the execution-chain facts and complete the legal classification before proceeding. Any sanctions match must then be handled through both the Swiss sanctions and AMLA reporting analyses.
