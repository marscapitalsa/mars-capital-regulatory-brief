Swiss sanctions · Binding sectoral prohibitions

Switzerland introduces new Russia-related crypto and sectoral prohibitions

What changed: Switzerland implemented the remaining measures of the EU's 20th Russia sanctions package. Most changes took effect on 20 August 2026. For a Swiss IAM, the central new rules are Article 20a, which prohibits direct or indirect participation in transactions involving the crypto-assets and central-bank digital currencies in Annex 13a, and Article 20b, which prohibits a legal person - expressly including a financial institution - from transacting directly or indirectly with Russian-established crypto-service providers or platforms that enable crypto exchange or transfer. Annex 13a currently names A7A5, RUBx and the digital rouble. [S1] [S2, pp. 9 and 17-18]

Action plan

Applicability
Sanctions
Action required. The new Swiss prohibitions apply to the IAM as a Swiss legal person. The immediate exposure review is particularly relevant because the IAM serves Russian clients. It must determine whether any client instruction, managed account, payment, custodian, broker, exchange, wallet, token or other transaction chain involves A7A5, RUBx, the digital rouble, or a crypto-service provider or exchange/transfer platform established in Russia. Other parts of the package apply only if the IAM participates in the specified maritime, trade, financing, managed-security-service, grant or Russian-judgment situations. [S2, Articles 12c, 12e, 12h, 14h, 20a, 20b, 28e and 28g-28k]
Responsible person / function
Proposed internal allocation based on the IAM's current structure:
Primary counterparty and transaction-chain review: outsourced compliance/AML provider and Portfolio Management
Internal control and escalation: designated AML Officer. Portfolio, custody and operational-risk review: in-house Risk Manager. Legal classification, exceptions and SECO contact: in-house Legal Counsel. Approval of policy changes and oversight of material or unresolved exposure: Directors.
Due / trigger
Most provisions legally effective: 20 August 2026. The expanded goods restrictions take effect on 1 September 2026; the managed-security-services restriction on 21 September 2026; and specified LNG-terminal and LNG-tanker provisions on 1 January 2027. The Ordinance sets no separate completion date for an IAM's internal review. Internal completion date: To be set internally. [S2, p. 17, Section III]
Status
Implementation review required
  • Identify crypto and payment-chain exposure. Check whether the IAM itself, any managed account or any client instruction uses A7A5, RUBx, the digital rouble, a Russian-established crypto-service provider, or a Russian-established exchange or transfer platform. Include indirect execution through a broker, custodian, wallet provider, payment service or other intermediary. Article 20b is categorical and does not depend on the platform being separately named in an annex. [S2, Articles 20a and 20b; Annex 13a]
  • Stop an in-scope transaction unless an exception is available. Article 20a prohibits direct and indirect participation in the specified crypto or digital-currency transactions and support for their development. Article 20b prohibits direct and indirect transactions by legal persons with the described Russian providers and platforms. The express exception in Article 20b(3) concerns official diplomatic, consular and qualifying international-organisation activity, not ordinary client business. [S2, Articles 20a and 20b]
  • Use the divestment route only after legal review. Article 30csepties permits SECO, after consultation with the competent authorities, to authorise an exception to Article 20b where required to withdraw investments from Russia or terminate business activities there. It is an authorisation route, not an automatic exception. [S2, Article 30csepties, p. 14]
  • Check the rest of the package against actual services and holdings. Review whether the IAM provides financial services or financing connected with covered tankers, icebreakers, LNG terminals, controlled goods or the Kyrgyzstan anti-circumvention restriction; supplies managed security services to the Russian government or Russian-established entities; receives covered Russian-government support; or transacts with a person captured by new Articles 28i-28k. These provisions should not be converted into blanket portfolio restrictions without the relevant statutory nexus. [S1] [S2, Articles 12c, 12e, 12h, 14h, 28e and 28g-28k]
  • Update internal transaction restrictions where exposure exists. Communicate any confirmed restriction to Portfolio Management and the relevant bank, broker, custodian or execution provider before a new instruction is accepted. This is a proposed internal control response to the binding prohibition; the Ordinance does not prescribe a particular policy format. [S2, Articles 20a and 20b]
Evidence
The Ordinance does not prescribe a special evidence format for an IAM's implementation review. The internal record can retain the services, accounts, instruments, platforms and intermediaries checked; the list and legal-text version used; any identified nexus; the resulting restriction or no-exposure conclusion; and any legal or director approval. This is a proposed internal control response.

What matters most for the IAM

  • Russian crypto platforms are now a Swiss-law issue. Article 20b applies to transactions with Russian-established crypto-service providers and platforms, whether the platform is named on a sanctions list or not. [S2, Article 20b]
  • Three crypto or digital-currency references are named. Annex 13a lists A7A5, RUBx and the digital rouble for Article 20a. [S2, Annex 13a, p. 18]
  • The rule reaches indirect transactions. Both Articles 20a and 20b use direct/indirect wording for legal persons, so the review must consider the execution and payment chain rather than the client's immediate counterparty only. [S2, Articles 20a and 20b]
  • Other measures are activity-specific. The package also adds restrictions involving vessels, LNG, sensitive exports through Kyrgyzstan, managed security services, Russian-government support and certain Russian judgments or intellectual-property measures. Their relevance depends on the IAM's actual services or transaction exposure. [S1] [S2]

Practical conclusion for the IAM

The immediate task is not another name-only rescreen. It is a product, provider and transaction-chain assessment under new categorical Swiss prohibitions. If the IAM and all managed accounts have no crypto-assets, wallets, Russian crypto providers or related payment rails, that no-exposure conclusion can close the principal IAM-specific review. If any such touchpoint exists, the transaction must be classified under Articles 20a and 20b before it proceeds. The remaining sectoral measures require a separate check only where the stated maritime, trade, services, financing or Russian-judgment nexus exists.
