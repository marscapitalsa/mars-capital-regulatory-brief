FINMA · 10 Nov · AML / Sanctions / Outsourcing

AMLO-FINMA specifies organisational sanctions controls

What changed: The adopted revision adds Article 30 and applies specified monitoring, internal-instruction, compliance, training and outsourcing requirements by analogy to preventing breaches of Swiss sanctions under the Embargo Act. [S1]

Why it matters: High for the Swiss IAM’s sanctions framework, especially where clients, beneficial owners or payment chains have exposure to restricted parties or activities.

An organisational rule, not a new sanctions list

Article 30 requires measures to prevent breaches of coercive measures under the Embargo Act and applies the provisions it lists by analogy. FINMA’s explanation links this to revised Article 8 AMLA. It does not replace the separate ordinances that determine who is listed, what is prohibited and whether an authorisation is available. [S1, Art. 30] [S2, section 4.5]

Pre-execution and ongoing controls

The explanatory report calls for monitoring that permits compliance with the Embargo Act, including ex-ante checks of relationships and transactions. Payment controls cover outgoing and incoming payments and the relevant parties, including originators, beneficiaries and other involved intermediaries where relevant. The report expressly discusses regular IT-supported screening for banks and securities firms; it should not be converted into an identical systems requirement for every smaller IAM. Design the IAM’s arrangements around its actual role and risks. [S2, section 4.5, pp. 12–14]

Outsourcing does not remove the organisational question

The listed provisions extend by analogy to internal instructions, specialist functions, risk analysis, training and the use of third parties. As an application to the IAM, check which actor actually updates lists, examines ownership and control, screens an intended instruction and escalates a potential restriction. A custodian’s transaction check and the IAM’s own review are not automatically the same task. [S1, Art. 30] [S2, section 4.5, pp. 13–15]

No permission to wait until January

FINMA states that supervised intermediaries already have to identify, monitor and limit sanctions risks under existing risk-management requirements. The 1 January commencement is therefore not a deferral of current bans, freezes or reporting duties. [S2, section 4.5] [S3]

Proposed execution: designated AML Officer and outsourced compliance/AML provider map controls and delegated tasks. In-house Legal Counsel: applicable Swiss restrictions. In-house Risk Manager: control review. Directors: oversight and approval of material policy or outsourcing changes under internal delegations.
Due / trigger
Effective 1 January 2027 · existing sanctions duties continue now · internal review: To be set internally. [S1]
Status
Applicability / gap review
  1. Compare current sanctions-related risk analysis, instructions, training, monitoring and delegated controls with Article 30 and the explanatory report; identify gaps relevant to the IAM’s activities. [S1] [S2]
  2. Clarify responsibility for pre-execution checks, ongoing monitoring and escalation where functions are split between the IAM, compliance provider and custodian. Apply the requirements for third parties where tasks are delegated. [S1] [S2]
Evidence
Proposed internal completion record: Current control allocation, identified gaps and the resulting policy, training or delegation updates where needed.
