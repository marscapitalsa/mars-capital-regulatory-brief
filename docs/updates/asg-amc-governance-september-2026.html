Planned AMC: document the product, roles and lifecycle before launch

What changed: an ASG partner article describes the institutionalisation of actively managed certificates and the resulting importance of issuer infrastructure, governance, transparency and lifecycle control. It is industry analysis, not a new legal rule. Because the IAM plans an AMC, the article is immediately relevant when read together with FINMA Guidance 03/2026 on products used in individual portfolio management. [S1] [S2]

Firm-specific application. The action trigger comes from the planned launch, not from a new deadline in the ASG article. Product approval should be completed before the IAM appoints the issuer, accepts rebalancing authority or recommends, distributes or places the certificate for a client.

Action plan

Applicability
AMC Products Conflicts
Applies to the planned AMC and to any mandate in which the IAM manages the strategy, recommends the certificate, distributes or places it, or invests client portfolios in it.
Responsible person / function
Primary execution: Portfolio Management and in-house Legal Counsel
Independent liquidity, concentration, valuation and operational challenge: in-house Risk Manager. Conduct and conflict review: designated AML/Compliance Officer and outsourced compliance provider. Product approval and risk-acceptance oversight: Directors or the formally designated approval body.
Due / trigger
Before issuer appointment, final product approval and first client use. No new external deadline is created by the article. Internal milestones: To be set internally.
Status
Pre-launch review required
  • Role map: state who is the issuer and product manufacturer, who sets and changes the strategy or index, who gives rebalancing instructions, who executes them, and who recommends or distributes the certificate.
  • Target market and client use: define the intended investor category, knowledge and experience, loss-bearing capacity, investment horizon, concentration tolerance and any jurisdictional distribution limits.
  • Underlying eligibility: specify permitted and excluded assets, leverage and derivatives, liquidity requirements, independent price sources and exceptions. The ASG article describes liquid and independently priceable underlyings as an agreed industry principle; FINMA separately expects liquidity, valuation, concentration and product-risk controls.
  • Methodology and discretion: document the strategy, investment limits, rebalancing authority, frequency, exceptional changes, benchmark or calculation rules and the control applied before an instruction reaches the issuer.
  • Issuer due diligence: assess credit risk, booking entity, collateral or segregation structure if any, hedging and execution model, calculation agent, secondary-market commitment, corporate-action handling, business continuity, termination events and replacement options.
  • Liquidity and valuation: test whether the certificate and its underlyings can be valued and exited under normal and stressed conditions; identify pricing sources, spreads, market-making assumptions, suspension events and the effect on individual mandates.
  • Conflicts and remuneration: identify issuer payments, management or strategy fees, retrocessions, spread or execution benefits, affiliated products and any circumstance in which the IAM acts both as strategy manager and distributor. Define disclosure and mitigation before use.
  • Client documents: ensure that the term sheet, key information, mandate and suitability record explain issuer credit risk, fees, liquidity, valuation, strategy discretion and the absence of fund-style investor ownership of underlying assets.
  • Lifecycle control: assign monitoring for limits, liquidity, valuation, issuer credit, concentration, performance anomalies, conflicts, complaints, rebalancing failures and termination or migration.
Evidence
Approved product memorandum; role and responsibility matrix; issuer due-diligence file; term sheet and strategy rules; target-market and jurisdiction analysis; liquidity, valuation and concentration assessment; conflict and remuneration analysis; client disclosure and suitability template; rebalancing-control procedure; monitoring dashboard; and Directors' or approval-body decision.

What the ASG article says

  • AMCs are generally treated in Switzerland as structured products rather than collective investment schemes and are debt securities issued by a financial institution. [S1]
  • The article identifies liquid and independently priceable underlyings, documented methodology and rebalancing authority, investor understanding of issuer risk and robust lifecycle oversight as core market principles. [S1]
  • It also highlights possible role overlap between manufacturer, external AMC manager and distributor, particularly where the AMC manager also distributes the product. [S1]
  • It recommends selecting issuers with strong infrastructure, transparent operating frameworks and robust risk management. That is the author's industry recommendation, not a FINMA order. [S1]

How FINMA changes the practical standard

FINMA Guidance 03/2026 is supervisory guidance prompted by cases involving unsuitable, illiquid, concentrated and insufficiently reviewed products. It expects risk identification and governance before products are used in individual portfolio management and focuses on due diligence, liquidity, valuation, concentration, conflicts and ongoing monitoring. The detailed AMC checklist above combines those supervisory themes with the operating points in the ASG article; the specific internal documents and approval route are an IAM-specific implementation proposal.
