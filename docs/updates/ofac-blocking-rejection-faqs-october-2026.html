OFAC updates its guidance on blocking, rejecting and reporting

What changed: OFAC refreshed 11 FAQs on when to block rather than reject, how to handle blocked accounts and how to report. The update explains the existing reporting framework; it is not a new Swiss filing rule. [S1] [S2]

Relevance to a Swiss IAM. Medium; high in an affected payment chain. Identify which institution took the action and whether the IAM has its own obligation instead of treating every payment refusal as a US rejection.

Block versus reject

A block immobilises property in which a blocked person has an interest. If a transaction is prohibited but there is no blockable interest under the applicable programme, the institution may have to reject it instead. These are different from a purely commercial refusal under a provider’s policy. [S1] [S2, FAQs 32, 35–36]

Which filing is required

The FAQs explain that reports of blocked property and rejected transactions must be filed through the OFAC Reporting System within 10 business days where required, with relevant supporting documents. Sections 501.603 and 501.604 identify who must file and what the reports contain. [S2, FAQs 41–42 and 49] [S3]

Applicability to be confirmed

Application to the IAM

  1. Where an affected payment is stopped, confirm with the relevant bank or custodian whether the action is a block, a rejection or a provider-policy refusal, and identify the governing programme. [S2]
  2. Assess the IAM’s own reporting role and preserve the supporting payment instructions if the reporting rule applies; do not assume the US report replaces any Swiss obligation. [S2] [S3]
Evidence
The payment-chain institution’s response, legal classification of the action, reporting allocation and filing record where applicable.
