FINMA · 10 Nov · AML

AMLO-FINMA expressly requires understanding ownership and control

What changed: The adopted revision adds Article 9b: financial intermediaries must understand the counterparty’s ownership and control structure. FINMA describes this as expressing an existing principle, not a blanket new re-identification exercise. [S1]

Why it matters: High for corporate clients, trusts and multi-layered structures. A beneficial-owner name alone may not explain who ultimately controls the client and how.

What changes — and what does not

Article 9b expressly states the requirement to understand the counterparty’s ownership and control structure. FINMA explains that this complements the requirements to understand domiciliary companies and assess complex structures. It provides a general basis for correctly identifying and verifying beneficial owners and controlling persons. The explanatory report says the principle is already applied in supervisory practice; it does not describe a new general requirement to redo all client identification. [S1, Art. 9b] [S2, section 4.3, pp. 9–11]

What the IAM may need to clarify

For a client with several entity layers, identify the actual natural-person controllers and how control is exercised. Where a trust sits above an underlying company, the report uses that arrangement as an example of why the structure must be understood. A diagram can help for complex ownership and control relationships; FINMA does not require a diagram for every client. The information and inquiries must be proportionate to risk and documented. [S2, section 4.3, pp. 10–11]

Practical application

For an existing corporate mandate, compare the identified beneficial owner or controlling person with the supporting ownership chain and governance rights. If the file merely names an individual without explaining a complex structure, determine what further information is needed. If the existing documentation already makes a simple structure and actual control clear, the new provision is not itself a reason to request the same documents again. [S1] [S2]
