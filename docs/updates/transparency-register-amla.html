Swiss corporate transparency · Binding legislation

LETA and the revised AMLA require the IAM to prepare its own beneficial-owner filing

What changed: the Act on the Transparency of Legal Entities (LETA/TJPG), the revised AMLA and their implementing ordinances enter into force on 1 October 2026. A Swiss AG must identify and document its beneficial owners and file the required information with the non-public federal register operated by the Federal Office of Justice, subject to the applicable transition date. [S1] [S2] [S3]

Action plan

Applicability
UBO register Corporate reporting AMLA
Action required. The IAM's Swiss AG is within the corporate reporting framework. Its own filing must be treated separately from AMLA due diligence on clients. [S1] [S3]
Responsible person / function
Proposed internal allocation:
Primary preparation and filing: in-house Legal Counsel
Corporate records and ownership information: Directors and the relevant shareholders or beneficial owners. Statutory responsibility rests with the highest-ranking member of the governing body; tasks may be delegated, but responsibility remains. Filing approval and oversight: Directors. The outsourced compliance/AML provider should be consulted only where the corporate analysis affects AMLA files or a discrepancy is identified. [S3]
Due / trigger
Effective date: 1 October 2026.
First commercial-register change after that date: file within one month of the change.
Swiss AG subject to ordinary audit: statutory backstop 1 January 2027.
Swiss AG not subject to ordinary audit: statutory backstop 1 March 2027.
A 1 October 2028 simplified deadline applies only where every beneficial owner is already entered in the commercial register as a shareholder or member of the governing body and the other statutory conditions are met.
Internal preparation target: To be set internally, but no later than the first applicable statutory trigger or backstop. [S3, transition timetable]
Status
Implementation required
  • Confirm the audit category immediately. Record whether the AG is subject to ordinary audit. This determines whether the normal backstop is 1 January or 1 March 2027; do not use 1 February as a catch-all date for an AG. [S3, transition timetable]
  • Map the control chain. Identify each natural person who directly or indirectly holds at least 25% of capital or voting rights or otherwise exercises control. If no such person can be identified, record the highest-ranking member of the governing body under the statutory fallback. [S3, “Identifying beneficial owners”]
  • Prepare the reportable dataset and supporting file. For each beneficial owner, collect the required identifying data and document the nature and extent of control. Obtain the necessary information from shareholders and beneficial owners and resolve inconsistencies before filing. [S2] [S3]
  • Prepare access to the filing channel. Confirm who will be authorised to submit through the official register interface or EasyGov and complete the account and representation steps before the applicable filing trigger. [S4] [S5]
  • File by the earliest applicable date. A commercial-register change after 1 October creates the one-month filing trigger even if the category backstop is later. Use the 2028 simplified route only after Legal Counsel documents that all of its conditions are satisfied. [S3]
  • Maintain the record after the initial filing. Report a change within one month after the company becomes aware of it and retain the underlying beneficial-owner documentation for ten years after the relevant person ceases to be a beneficial owner. [S3, ongoing duties]
  • Keep company reporting distinct from client AMLA controls. The enacted framework does not create a systematic obligation for every financial intermediary to query the register for every client. Existing AMLA identification, clarification and reporting duties continue independently. [S2, discrepancy mechanism commentary]
Evidence
Audit-category confirmation; approved ownership and control chart; beneficial-owner identification and supporting documents; record of the applicable statutory trigger and deadline; filing authority; submission confirmation; and a procedure for one-month change reporting and ten-year record retention. The detailed internal allocation and file format are proposed controls; the statutory duties and dates are stated above.

What matters

  • The current IAM-specific deadline cannot be fixed without its audit status. For a Swiss AG the ordinary-audit branch leads to 1 January 2027; the non-ordinary-audit branch leads to 1 March 2027, unless an earlier commercial-register-change trigger or the narrowly available simplified route applies. [S3]
  • The register is federal and non-public. The Federal Office of Justice operates it, while the federal control office checks completeness, accuracy and currency. [S1]
  • Corporate filing is not a substitute for AMLA due diligence. The IAM must maintain its own corporate transparency record and continue the separate AMLA analysis for client relationships. [S1] [S2]

Practical conclusion for the IAM

The immediate decision is the AG's audit category and whether a commercial-register change is expected after 1 October. Once those facts are confirmed, Legal Counsel can set the exact filing date, complete the ownership-and-control map, prepare the filing authority and submit through the official channel. A deadline of 1 February 2027 should not be assigned to the IAM merely because it is an AG: that date applies to a different category in the transition timetable.
