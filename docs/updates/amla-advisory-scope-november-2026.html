FINMA Guidance 06/2026 · 10 November 2026 · AML

AMLA advisory activities: which IAM services are in scope?

What changed: FINMA clarified how Circular 2011/1 must be read after AMLA expanded to cover specified advisory services on 1 October 2026. Activities outside financial intermediation are not necessarily outside AMLA. For an existing SO-monitored IAM, an in-scope advisory service may require notification to its supervisory organisation by 1 December 2026, rather than a new SRO application. [S1, Arts. 2 and 12a] [S2, transitional provision] [S3, sections 2–3]

Why this matters for an IAM. The question is not whether the engagement is called “advisory”, or whether the IAM holds the client’s money. The question is whether its actual work professionally contributes to a specified transaction or provides a qualifying domicile service. Corporate structuring, creating an investment vehicle, arranging contributions or distributions, and helping with an acquisition can need a separate assessment alongside the IAM’s ordinary portfolio-management business. [S1, Art. 2(3bis)–(3ter)] [S2, Arts. 12d–12f]

What matters

  • Listed activity, not every kind of advice. There must be the relevant connection to one of the five legal operations in Article 2(3bis), or the separate long-term domicile service in Article 2(3ter). General investment commentary is not automatically captured by those categories. [S1]
  • No client-money mandate is required for the advisory test. Advice that causally contributes to the specified legal operation can qualify, even without authority to execute the transfer. Where the same activity is also financial intermediation, the financial-intermediary rules take precedence. [S1, Art. 2b] [S2, Art. 12d]
  • Domestic and foreign entity formation differ. The formation limb covers a non-operating entity in Switzerland, but an entity abroad need not be non-operating to fall within that limb. [S1, Art. 2(3bis)(b)]
  • The professional thresholds are alternatives, not a checklist of cumulative conditions. Staying below them does not automatically exclude a business carried on independently for continuing income. [S2, Art. 12f]
  • Check statutory exemptions against the actual operation. In particular, the exemption for a transfer below CHF 5 million requires the purchase price to be paid and received exclusively through AMLA-subject banks or other financial intermediaries. It is not a general exemption for advice about assets below that value. [S1, Art. 2(4ter)(b)]
  • Existing AML status is not a blanket exclusion. A mixed business must classify its activities. An SO-monitored portfolio manager remains under the relevant SO for its advisory activities. [S1, Arts. 2b and 12a(3)]
  • The relevant transitional deadline is 1 December 2026 if the financial intermediary was already carrying on the covered advisory service on 1 October. It is not 1 January 2027, the start date of a separate AMLO-FINMA revision. [S2, transitional provision, para. 2] [S5]

Scope tests · Exemptions · Practical cases · Duties · Supervision · Deadlines · Action plan

1. Apply the scope tests to the service actually provided

A. Is the service carried on in or from Switzerland?

The Federal Council’s AMLO applies to advisers acting in or from Switzerland. Identify which legal person or individual supplies the service, where that work is carried out, and whether the counterparty is a third-party client. The client’s nationality alone does not decide this test. [S2, Art. 2(1)]

B. Does it contribute to a specific financial transaction?

Article 12d AMLO includes advice that causally contributes to a legal operation connected with one of the financial transactions listed below; the statute expressly includes fundraising. As an application to the IAM, distinguish general education or an introduction from work that helps design, negotiate, prepare or implement the specific operation. The label “introduction” is not conclusive if the actual role is more substantial. A merely administrative contact or general discussion should not be assumed to meet the causal test without examining the facts. [S1, Art. 2(3bis)] [S2, Art. 12d]

The five statutory categories are:

  1. Buying or selling real estate. AMLO also covers operations economically equivalent to a transfer of the power of disposal and the creation of a usufruct or building right for consideration. For buying or selling real estate, Article 12e(3) identifies the trigger as both parties expressing their intention to conclude the purchase agreement, absent a statutory exemption. [S1, Art. 2(3bis)(a)] [S2, Art. 12e]
  2. Forming or establishing a non-operating Swiss entity, or an entity abroad. Do not read “non-operating” into the foreign-entity limb where the statutory wording does not do so. Assistance in forming a foreign operating subsidiary can therefore require review even where forming a genuinely operating Swiss company would not fall under this particular limb. [S1, Art. 2(3bis)(b)]
  3. Managing or administering a non-operating entity. Check the function performed for the entity rather than equating it with managing a securities account in that entity’s name. These are not necessarily the same activity. [S1, Arts. 2(3bis)(c) and 2b]
  4. Contributions to, and distributions from, a non-operating entity. Structuring a contribution or distribution may fall within the advisory limb even if a separate bank executes the payment. If the IAM itself executes in a way that constitutes financial intermediation, apply the coordination rule instead of treating the activity as unregulated advice. [S1, Arts. 2(3bis)(d) and 2b] [S2, Art. 12d]
  5. Buying or selling an entity where a non-operating entity carries out the purchase or sale. Check the buyer, seller, transaction object and vehicle rather than presuming all M&A advice, or every purchase of a financial instrument, is covered. [S1, Art. 2(3bis)(e)]

C. Separately: are addresses or premises supplied as a domicile for more than six months?

Article 2(3ter) separately covers professionally providing addresses or premises as the domicile or registered office of legal entities for more than six months. This route is not dependent on identifying one of the five transactions above. Exactly six months is not “more than six months”; the arrangement and actual duration must be checked rather than inferred from a single invoice. An ordinary lease should not be equated automatically with a professional domicile service without checking the service’s purpose and facts. [S1, Art. 2(3ter)]

D. Is the service professional?

The general test is an independent economic activity directed towards continuing income. Main-business and side-business activities are treated alike. Under Article 12f(1), the activity is professional in any event if any one of the following applies. For the asset and transaction-volume criteria, the provision uses what the adviser must assume when exercising the diligence appropriate to the circumstances. [S2, Art. 12f]

Alternative professional-activity criteria — Article 12f AMLO
CriterionThreshold in the provisionPractical check
Advisory gross revenueMore than CHF 50,000 per calendar yearRevenue, not profit. A low standalone invoice is not conclusive.
Clients or legal operationsMore than 20 clients advised, or involvement in more than 20 legal operations, per calendar yearEither count suffices; the two counts are not cumulative conditions.
Third-party assets concernedMore than CHF 5 million at any pointAssess the assets to which the advisory service relates; authority to dispose of them is not an express condition here.
Financial transactions concernedTotal volume exceeding CHF 2 million per calendar yearReview the transactions the advice concerns, not merely the fee charged.

Below the numbers does not mean automatically outside. The opening general test remains relevant. Nor should the IAM mechanically use all of its portfolio-management clients or total AUM as the advisory counts: first identify the relevant service and what the advice concerns. [S2, Art. 12f]

Close persons have a specific rule. Under Article 12f(3), work for close persons counts towards professional activity only if its annual gross revenue exceeds CHF 50,000. Paragraph 4 defines these as relatives and relatives by marriage in the direct line; relatives up to the third collateral degree; spouses and registered partners; co-heirs until division of the estate; reversionary heirs and legatees under Article 488 Civil Code; and a person living with the adviser in a lasting partnership. This is not an exemption for all clients of a family office, for the families of every employee, or for an unrelated corporate client. [S2, Art. 12f(3)–(4)]

2. Determine whether an exemption actually applies

What is a non-operating entity?

Article 2a(6) includes legal persons, companies, establishments, foundations, trusts, fiduciary undertakings and similar arrangements that are not formed or maintained to operate, or support the operating activities of, a business or group. Domiciliary companies are expressly included. A holding-company, SPV or trust label is not a sufficient conclusion: check its purpose and actual function, including any support for operating activities. [S1, Art. 2a(6)]

The transaction-specific low-risk exemptions

Article 2(4ter) lists the following exclusions. Apply their conditions to the relevant task; do not turn them into a blanket exclusion for the provider’s other services. [S1, Art. 2(4ter)]

  • Transactions concerning real estate or entities arising from family, matrimonial or matrimonial-property law, inheritance or gifts, or between connected persons as referred to in Article 2(2)(a) FinIA.
  • Transfers of real estate or entities valued below CHF 5 million, provided the purchase price is paid and received exclusively through banks or other financial intermediaries subject to AMLA. A value of exactly CHF 5 million does not satisfy “below”. Do not equate the size of an advisory fee with the value of the transfer.
  • Purchase of an owner-occupied home in Switzerland, or a Swiss replacement residential property within the cited tax-law definition. “Residential” alone does not establish owner occupation.
  • Transfers of agricultural businesses or land under the rural land law to persons intending to operate them themselves.
  • Transfers of land for land consolidation and similar operations.
  • Governing-body activities for operating entities and for charitable foundations and operating associations based in Switzerland. This is not a blanket exemption for all corporate-formation work.
  • Establishment of foundations on death.
  • Pure notarisation without ancillary advice.

Other provider and capacity exclusions. Article 2(4)(f) addresses lawyers and notaries acting in connection with judicial, criminal, administrative or arbitral proceedings, including the specified related advisory work. Article 2(4bis) excludes authorised or supervised audit providers for their audit work. Article 2(3) AMLO excludes intra-group services and specified qualifying auxiliary persons; auxiliary status requires the listed selection, instruction, control, training, payment and written-agreement conditions. Merely using a lawyer, calling someone an assistant or outsourcing compliance is not enough. [S1, Art. 2(4)(f), (4bis)] [S2, Art. 2(3)]

3. Practical IAM cases

These are illustrative applications of the statutory tests. The stated assumptions matter.

Portfolio allocation advice or investment commentary only
Advice about allocating a client’s liquid portfolio, without assistance in one of the listed legal operations or a domicile service, does not become a new Article 2(3bis) advisory service merely because it is called “investment advice”. Existing FinSA and financial-intermediation obligations must still be considered; executing investments can already be financial intermediation. [S1, Arts. 2(3)(f), 2(3bis) and 2b]
Helping a client form a foreign company
If the IAM’s work professionally contributes to formation connected with a financial transaction, the foreign-entity limb can apply even if the company will operate a business. Check the actual contribution and exemptions. A generic referral to an independent provider, without involvement in the operation, should be distinguished from designing the structure or coordinating its formation. [S1, Art. 2(3bis)(b)] [S2, Art. 12d]
Forming a Swiss operating company
The Swiss-formation limb refers to non-operating entities. Formation of a genuinely operating Swiss business does not fall under that limb merely by being formation work. Other services, such as a qualifying domicile service or financial intermediation, must be assessed separately. [S1, Art. 2(3bis)(b), (3ter) and 2a(6)]
Preparing an investment SPV or AMC issuer
The product label is not decisive. If the work includes forming a non-operating Swiss vehicle or a foreign entity, arranging its funding, or advising on contributions and distributions, the corresponding limbs need review. Merely holding an AMC or foreign fund unit does not by itself establish that the IAM provides one of those services. [S1, Art. 2(3bis)(b)–(e)] [S2, Art. 12d]
Administering a passive wealth-holding structure or trust
Assess whether it is non-operating under Article 2a(6), whether the role contributes to the relevant financial transactions, and whether the IAM is administering the entity rather than solely managing its investment portfolio. If the same conduct is also financial intermediation, Article 2b(1) determines the applicable duties. [S1, Arts. 2(3bis)(c)–(d), 2a(6) and 2b]
Assisting with a CHF 4 million property transfer
For the below-CHF-5-million exemption, confirm both the transfer value and that the full purchase price is paid and received through AMLA-subject intermediaries. Do not assume a foreign bank qualifies solely because it is regulated abroad. An investor’s rental property is not automatically an owner-occupied-home exemption. [S1, Art. 2(4ter)(b)–(c)]
Giving a client entity a registered address for eight months
A professional domicile arrangement for more than six months can fall under the separate Article 2(3ter) route without needing a property sale or incorporation transaction. Check any intra-group or qualifying auxiliary-person exclusion against all its conditions. [S1, Art. 2(3ter)] [S2, Art. 2(3)]
In-house legal work or an external professional’s involvement
Identify which person supplies the service and in what capacity. The IAM’s service is not automatically excluded because its in-house Legal Counsel works on it, an external lawyer drafts documents, or compliance is outsourced. Apply any professional, transaction or auxiliary-person exclusion to the actual provider and activity. [S1, Art. 2(4)(f), (4bis) and (4ter)] [S2, Art. 2(3)]

4. What follows if the advisory activity is covered?

Article 8b requires identification of the client, identification of the beneficial owner and documentation, using its references to Articles 3, 4 and 7. The adviser must establish the subject and purpose of the engagement and make additional inquiries into its background and purpose where justified by high risks. Article 8c makes the extent of diligence risk-based; Article 8d requires organisational measures, training and controls against money laundering, terrorist financing and breaches of Swiss sanctions. [S1, Arts. 8b–8d]

For the IAM, this may require supplementing an existing portfolio-management KYC file with the particular entity, parties, ownership, purpose and financial operation involved. It does not automatically require duplicating the whole client file or collecting a fixed set of documents for every matter. Record the inquiries so that a knowledgeable third party can understand the transaction, relationship and compliance assessment. Article 7 includes risk-based updating and a ten-year retention rule after the relationship ends or the transaction is completed. [S1, Arts. 7 and 8b]

MROS is not optional merely because advice does not involve custody. Article 9(1ter) requires an adviser to report without delay where the statutory knowledge or reasonable-suspicion conditions apply, including specified proceeds of crime, criminal or terrorist organisations, or terrorist financing. Ending negotiations because of the relevant suspicion is also covered. Article 9(1sexies) applies the reasonable-suspicion definition by analogy. The statutory rule for lawyers and notaries acting in that capacity has additional conditions in Article 9(2); it is not a universal exemption for an IAM’s legal department. Apply the relevant no-tipping-off provisions rather than telling the client that a report has been made. [S1, Art. 9(1ter), (1quinquies)–(2), Art. 10a]

5. Which rules and which supervisor for an existing IAM?

  • Same activity is both financial intermediation and covered advice: the financial-intermediary rules apply to that activity under Article 2b(1).
  • Different activities within one business: each activity follows its applicable substantive rules. Article 2b(2) permits an election to apply the financial-intermediary rules across the business. Article 2a AMLO requires a declaration to the competent supervisor describing the activity and organisational changes; it normally takes effect on the following 1 January, unless an earlier date is approved. Paragraph 5 also provides for analogous application of AMLO-FINMA to the covered Article 2(2)(a)–(dquater) intermediaries that have not made that declaration. For a portfolio manager, discuss the resulting implementation with its SO; do not assume that an existing licence makes advisory classification unnecessary.
  • SO-monitored portfolio manager or trustee: Article 12a(3) puts all activities under the relevant supervisory organisation. Guidance 06/2026 directs questions about advisory work by SO-monitored intermediaries to that organisation. Do not automatically apply the standalone adviser’s new SRO-membership route.
  • SRO-affiliated financial intermediary: Article 12a(2) provides supervision of its activities through that SRO. A directly FINMA-supervised intermediary follows Article 12a(1). A standalone adviser without financial-intermediary activity generally follows the recognised SRO route.

[S1, Arts. 2b, 12 and 12a] [S2, Art. 2a] [S3, section 3]

Notification route for an SO-monitored IAM. The older FINMA web notice refers broadly to FINMA-supervised or licensed firms notifying FINMA. The more specific statutory rule in Article 12a(3), the AMLO transitional provision and Guidance 06/2026 direct the analysis to the competent SO for an SO-monitored portfolio manager. Confirm that SO’s submission route and any required coordination with FINMA rather than assuming that the generic notice changes the statutory supervisor. [S1, Art. 12a(3)] [S2, transitional provision, para. 2] [S3, section 3] [S4]

The old circular is not a safe harbour. Guidance 06/2026 limits Circular 2011/1 to financial intermediation. A service previously described as not being financial intermediation must still be tested against the new advisory provisions. FINMA identifies conflicting passages for future amendment, including margins 3, 60, 96, 110–132 and 133–141. [S3, section 2]

6. Deadlines depend on when and in what capacity the service started

Statutory routes — AMLO transitional provision and Article 12g
SituationRequired route and timing
Existing financial intermediary carrying on covered advice on 1 October 2026Notify the competent authority or supervisory organisation by 1 December 2026. For an SO-monitored IAM, determine the filing route with the relevant SO.
Standalone adviser already carrying on covered advice on 1 October 2026Apply to join an SRO by 1 December 2026. Pending the decision, the transitional provision limits continued activity to existing business relationships.
Later change to professional covered advisory activityComply immediately with the duties listed in Article 12g(1)(a), and apply for SRO affiliation or notify the competent supervisor within two months of the change, according to the applicable route. For the SRO-affiliation route, paragraph 2 permits continued advice to existing clients and new matters from them pending affiliation. Missing the required filing or being refused affiliation prohibits continuing the covered activity.

[S2, Art. 12g and transitional provision, paras. 1–2] [S3]

The November guidance does not restart these periods or postpone substantive duties to January 2027. If the review finds no covered advisory activity, the notification provision is not a blanket requirement to file a nil return; record the activity-by-activity conclusion as an internal review outcome. Any internal date not fixed externally is To be set internally. [S1, Art. 2] [S2]

If the covered advisory business later stops: where the provider remains a financial intermediary, Article 12i requires notification of the cessation to its competent authority or organisation within two months. This is distinct from the deadline for starting the activity. [S2, Art. 12i]
