ASG · Association support · 26 August 2026

ASG publishes a model agreement for business introducers

What changed: ASG published an editable model agreement in French, German and Italian for intermediaries who bring prospective wealth-management or investment-advisory clients to an IAM. ASG describes it as a structured starting point, not a ready-to-sign standard: each manager must adapt it to its organisation, activities, services and client base. [S1] [S2, disclaimer and clauses 1–15]

IAM application. Because the IAM uses a business introducer, the publication warrants a documented comparison of the existing agreement with the model and with the relationship as it operates in practice. ASG identifies regulatory, civil-liability and employment-law risks, and warns that an introducer's fault—for example a cross-border breach or an incorrect assurance to a client—may be attributed directly to the portfolio manager. This is an internal implementation conclusion based on the disclosed use of an introducer; ASG does not prescribe an implementation deadline. [S1]

Action plan

Applicability
Introducers Cross-border AML Legal
Applies to each person or firm engaged by the IAM to introduce prospective clients, including a relationship in which the IAM pays the fee or commission. The model assumes that the introducer's service is limited to introductions. [S2, preamble and clauses 1–2, 8]
Responsible person / function
Proposed internal allocation:
Primary: Director responsible for the introducer relationship
Contract, cross-border and data review: in-house Legal Counsel. AML/KYC boundaries and monitoring: designated AML Officer and outsourced compliance/AML provider. Risk challenge: in-house Risk Manager. Final appointment, material amendment and continued-use decision: Directors.
Due / trigger
No external deadline stated. Review date: To be set internally. Complete the review before entering into a new agreement or materially changing the introducer's role, countries, remuneration, ownership, personnel or access to client information. ASG recommends legal advice for substantial changes to its model. [S1]
Status
Review required
  • Map the actual relationship. Record the introducer's legal identity, domicile, ownership where relevant, individual contacts, target client countries, exact steps performed, information exchanged, fee calculation and any use of the IAM's name or materials.
  • Compare conduct with the permitted role. Confirm whether the introducer does more than establish contact, present a prospect or recommend a prospect to the IAM. Any broader activity must be identified before the model is adopted. [S2, clauses 1–3]
  • Retain IAM control of onboarding. Client acceptance, service selection, pricing and identification remain with the IAM. The model prohibits the introducer from performing AML checks and states that prospects pass through the IAM's onboarding process without exception. [S2, clauses 2, 5 and 7]
  • Complete the cross-border analysis. For each prospect jurisdiction, determine which approaches and communications are permitted. The model requires the introducer to know and follow the applicable cross-border rules, consult the IAM and refrain from active approaches when uncertain, and avoid a permanent physical presence abroad. [S2, clauses 4 and 6]
  • Set remuneration precisely. Select and complete the fee formula, payment frequency, duration, cessation on client termination and treatment of tax, VAT and social-security deductions. Confirm how the remuneration arrangement is disclosed to introduced clients. [S2, clause 8]
  • Test independent status where the introducer is an individual. The agreement's label does not itself prevent employment reclassification. The Swiss social-insurance wording in the model is expressly limited to a natural person domiciled in Switzerland; foreign arrangements require jurisdiction-specific analysis. [S1] [S2, clauses 4 and 8]
  • Set information boundaries. Define what prospect information may be transferred, the required notices or consents, confidentiality, access, retention and return or destruction. The model states that the IAM may disclose information about introduced clients only with their express written consent, subject to legal disclosure duties. [S2, clause 10]
  • Complete the lifecycle provisions. Fill every placeholder for term, notice, post-termination remuneration and non-solicitation; confirm breach escalation, immediate termination, indemnity, non-assignment, governing law and forum. [S2, clauses 3 and 9–15]
Evidence
Approved relationship map; clause-by-clause comparison; signed adapted agreement; jurisdiction-specific cross-border assessment; remuneration schedule and client-disclosure wording; AML/onboarding responsibility matrix; independence and authority documents where applicable; and approval or review record showing the functions involved. The precise internal evidence package is a proposed implementation approach; the ASG model itself does not prescribe a file format.

Contract perimeter: what the introducer may and may not do

  • Permitted core: bring the IAM a prospective natural or legal person who has not previously had a contractual relationship with it. The contemplated activity is limited to making contact and presenting or recommending the prospect to the IAM. [S2, clauses 1–2]
  • No representation or agency: the introducer may not act in the IAM's name or for its account, bind the IAM, prepare or submit offers, or present itself as the IAM's representative, agent or employee. [S2, clauses 2 and 4]
  • No client advice or product distribution: the model prohibits advising introduced clients and marketing, distributing or promoting financial instruments managed or advised by the IAM. It also prohibits recommending or promoting the IAM's services on its behalf. [S2, clause 2]
  • No AML delegation: the introducer may not perform the IAM's AML checks. The introducer undertakes not to refer a prospect with suspicious source of funds, to communicate relevant AML information promptly and to cooperate with competent authorities, but the IAM retains its own identification and onboarding responsibilities. [S2, clauses 2, 5 and 7]
  • No unauthorised brand use: the introducer may not use the IAM's company name, trade name, brand, logo, website or business cards for advertising. [S2, clause 2]

Points that cannot be copied without a decision

  • Fee mechanics: the model offers alternatives—percentage of management/advisory fees, a fixed amount, an amount linked to introduced assets, or an AuM commission—and leaves duration and payment frequency blank. These choices must match the actual commercial arrangement. [S2, clause 8]
  • Swiss individual introducers: the social-insurance clause contemplates a current compensation-office certificate before remuneration is treated as non-salary. It also contains repayment or set-off wording if an authority later treats the introducer as an employee. Its use requires review against the actual status and domicile. [S2, clause 8]
  • Termination economics: the model's main formulation ends the fee entitlement when the introducer agreement or the introduced-client relationship ends, but it also supplies an alternative continuing-fee period. One formulation must be selected and completed. [S2, clauses 8 and 12]
  • Non-solicitation: the duration and any contractual penalty are placeholders rather than ASG-selected terms. [S2, clause 9]
  • Foreign-based introducers: clause 6 contains a broad prohibition on the introducer operating through a permanent physical presence abroad. Where an introducer is established or works outside Switzerland, that wording cannot be adopted mechanically; Legal Counsel should assess the actual location, target markets and applicable foreign rules before adapting it. This is an IAM application of the model's wording, not a separate rule stated by ASG. [S2, clause 6]
  • Language: ASG states that the model is available in French, German and Italian, but not English. Any English version used operationally should therefore be treated as an IAM-prepared adaptation rather than the ASG model. [S1]

How to close the review

  1. Legal Counsel marks the existing contract against all 15 sections of the ASG model and the explanatory PwC checklist.
  2. The relationship Director confirms that the written description matches actual communications, referrals, payments and data flows.
  3. The AML Officer and outsourced provider confirm that the introducer is not being used as an unapproved AML/KYC delegate and that relevant information reaches the IAM's onboarding process.
  4. Legal Counsel documents the permitted approach for each country in which prospects are contacted.
  5. Risk Manager records unresolved deviations and the control response.
  6. Directors approve the adapted agreement and any decision to continue a relationship with material deviations.

This workflow is a proposed internal allocation for the IAM. It is not stated as a mandatory sequence in the ASG documents.
