Lenz & Staehelin · Legal analysis · 17 September 2026

Private assets: turn illiquidity, valuation and investor-status risks into product controls

What changed: the publication is a legal analysis of existing Swiss duties, not a new rule. It examines the practical consequences of investing in private assets through direct investments and foreign private funds, including competence, capital calls, nominee structures, transfer restrictions, valuation, conflicts and qualified-investor status. [S1]

IAM relevance. The IAM has disclosed illiquid assets and foreign funds. The paper is therefore useful for checking existing product and mandate files even though it does not create an external implementation deadline.

Conditional review plan

Applicability
Products Illiquid assets Foreign funds
Relevant to mandates that hold or may acquire private-equity, private-debt, venture, infrastructure, real-estate or similar illiquid interests, directly or through funds.
Responsible person / function
Primary: Portfolio Management
Contract, disclosure and investor-status review: in-house Legal Counsel. Liquidity, concentration and valuation challenge: Risk Manager. Conduct controls: outsourced compliance. Material product approval: Directors or the function designated by the IAM's governance.
Due / trigger
No external deadline created by the paper. Review before a new private-asset acquisition and for material existing exposures: To be set internally.
Status
Product-file review
  • Confirm that the IAM and relevant staff have the competence and organisation required for the asset class.
  • Document capital-call forecasting, cash or liquid-asset buffers and the consequences of a missed call.
  • Identify nominee or registered-holder arrangements and allocate information, voting, contribution and distribution responsibilities.
  • Record transfer restrictions, consent rights, lock-ups, secondary-market limits and the effect on client termination or portfolio transfer.
  • Explain valuation methodology, frequency, reliance on sponsor NAV and the limits of independent verification.
  • Assess fee and valuation conflicts where the IAM's remuneration depends on reported asset value.
  • Verify the client's qualified-investor treatment for foreign collective investments, required disclosures and any opt-out or loss of status when the long-term management or advisory relationship ends.
  • Confirm suitability, risk capacity, concentration and liquidity at client and total-portfolio level.
Evidence
Product due-diligence file; competence assessment; mandate and subscription documents; liquidity and capital-call analysis; transfer and exit analysis; valuation memorandum; conflict assessment; client disclosure; investor-status record; suitability analysis; and approval record.

Why the issues are connected

  • Capital calls: an apparently diversified mandate may still require a dedicated liquidity reserve if the client has binding unfunded commitments.
  • Termination: a client may terminate an IAM mandate while an underlying interest remains non-transferable; the mandate and operating process should address that possibility before investment.
  • Valuation: sponsor-supplied NAV can be the only workable value, but the limitation and potential lag should be transparent to the client and reflected in fee controls.
  • Foreign funds: a private client may be treated as a qualified investor because of a long-term discretionary or advisory relationship, but the classification, information provided and consequences of termination must be understood.
  • Suitability: illiquidity is not an isolated product feature; it affects risk capacity, concentration, cash needs and the client's ability to exit.
