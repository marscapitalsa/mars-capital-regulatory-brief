FINMA · 8 Oct

FINMA changes digital client identification from 1 November

What changed: FINMA revised Circular 2016/7: Swiss e-ID and QR-coded official documents may be used, specified online routes require liveness detection, and equivalent digital processes may verify residence. The revised supervisory practice takes effect on 1 November.

Effective 1 November 2026 · margin 54 provides a one-year technical transition for the qualified-signature route only

Action: High relevance if digital onboarding is used. Confirm the applicable method and provider readiness before the revised practice takes effect.

EU · 12 Oct

EU lists 826 Russian companies and 743 executives

What changed: The EU added 1,569 names under its Ukraine territorial-integrity regime: 826 companies in Russia’s military-industrial complex and 743 executives. The measures include an asset freeze and a ban on making funds or economic resources available.

EU measures effective 12 October 2026 · internal review: To be set internally

Relevance: High conditional relevance: the size of the addition makes a complete list refresh and client/UBO/issuer exposure review more useful than a search for a few prominent names.

What changed: Bank of America agreed to pay $11,662,930 for 475 apparent sanctions violations. OFAC identified blocked-bank routing, low priority for exact name-and-birth-date matches, watchlist-update delays and account-handling errors.

Potentially relevant
OFSI · 9 Oct

OFSI clarifies what happens to specific licences after delisting

What changed: New FAQ 205 explains that a specific licence concerning only a delisted person and no remaining sanctions is no longer needed; OFSI need not be asked to revoke it. If the person is listed again, the old licence does not reactivate.

Guidance published 9 October 2026 · apply when a delisting or relisting affects a specific licence

Relevance: A later relisting requires a new licence application. An old permission should not be treated as automatically revived.
