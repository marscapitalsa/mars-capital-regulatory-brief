Action required

The corporate transparency implementation is due to start on 1 October, while the two Swiss sanctions amendments are already in force. Each topic page separates statutory timing from any internal implementation date.

Action required
Switzerland · 01 Oct

LETA and revised AMLA: prepare the IAM's beneficial-owner filing

What changed: the new federal transparency-register regime enters into force on 1 October 2026. The IAM's Swiss AG must identify and document its beneficial owners and file to the non-public federal register under the applicable transition rule.

Effective 1 October 2026 · file within one month after the first subsequent commercial-register change · AG backstop: 1 January 2027 if subject to ordinary audit, otherwise 1 March 2027

Action: confirm the AG's audit category, map natural persons holding at least 25% or otherwise exercising control, prepare the filing authority and supporting documents, and set the earliest applicable statutory date. The 1 October 2028 simplified route is available only if its conditions are documented.

Action required
Switzerland · 20 Aug

Switzerland introduces new Russia-related crypto and sectoral rules

What changed: crypto-assets and uncertificated securities are now expressly included in “funds”; transactions involving A7A5, RUBx and the digital rouble are restricted; and a Swiss legal person is prohibited from transacting directly or indirectly with Russian-established crypto-service providers and exchange or transfer platforms. The package also contains sectoral restrictions, wind-down rules and defensive remedies.

General rules in force since 20 August 2026 · statutory stages and wind-down cut-offs: 1 and 21 September, 21 November 2026, 1 January and 25 January 2027

Action: hold and classify any affected crypto transaction before execution; request written exposure confirmation from relevant custodians, brokers and venues; include crypto in asset-freeze controls; and apply both the SECO and AMLA/MROS analysis to a sanctions match. Review the remaining measures only where the specified exposure exists.

Action required
Switzerland · 11 Aug

Switzerland adds 54 Russia-related sanctions targets

What changed: the official Swiss delta added nine individuals and 45 entities to Annex 8 and amended 11 existing records across Annexes 8 and 2. It took effect on 11 August at 23:00.

In force since 11 August 2026, 23:00 · freeze and do not make assets available when Article 15 applies · notify SECO without delay

Action: load the current SECO list, re-screen relevant clients, UBOs/controllers, holdings, issuers and transaction parties, resolve ownership or control matches, and run the separate Article 6/9 AMLA analysis required by FINMA. Internal completion target: to be set internally; it does not postpone an immediate legal duty.

Potentially relevant
FINMA · 12 Jan

Crypto in an individual mandate requires a custody and segregation assessment

What changed: FINMA Guidance 01/2026 applies Article 24(1) FinIO to crypto custody. The portfolio manager must assess the custodian's permitted status, prudential supervision, technical capability and bankruptcy segregation; limited legacy arrangements require all stated safeguards and explicit written client consent.

Current FINMA supervisory expectation · no stated transition deadline · existing-exposure review date: to be set internally

Relevant if: any discretionary mandate holds or may hold crypto, or the IAM sponsors or manages a foreign crypto product placed in client portfolios. If no exposure exists, document that conclusion and reassess before the trigger changes.

Potentially relevant
Switzerland · 17 Aug

SECO updates Annex 2 data and relaxes the Article 27a restriction on Yelo Bank

What changed: the Swiss update amended four individual and 606 entity records in Annex 2 and removed Yelo Bank from Annex 14a. The removal means Article 27a(2) no longer prohibits a transaction solely because the counterparty is Yelo Bank; it is a relaxation, not a new asset-freeze measure.

In force since 17 August 2026, 23:00 · internal verification date: to be set internally

Relevant if: the IAM finances or supports controlled-goods activity involving an Annex 2 end-user, or a client, payment, counterparty or service chain uses Yelo Bank. Any change to an existing block still requires checks for other Swiss, foreign and contractual restrictions.

Potentially relevant
EU / Lenz & Staehelin · 03 Aug

EU AI Act: current transparency rules and revised implementation dates

What changed: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 was published on 24 July and entered into force on 27 July. It moved the core Annex III high-risk requirements to 2 December 2027 and Annex I embedded-system requirements to 2 August 2028, while the Article 50 transparency timetable remained unchanged.

Applicable dates include 2 August and 2 December 2026 · later high-risk dates depend on classification

Relevant if: the IAM has an EU establishment or uses AI output in the EU, supplies an AI system into the EU, operates an EU-facing chatbot, publishes covered AI-generated content, or uses an Annex III high-risk use case. Ordinary internal drafting or analysis in Switzerland is not automatically high-risk.

Potentially relevant
UK · 06 Aug

UK adds six Russian banks, seven other targets and six vessels

What changed: the UK Sanctions List added 13 Russia-regime designations and six ship specifications. The designated banks include Ozon Bank, Roseximbank, Center-Invest, Realist Bank, Bank Stavr and Teleport Bank.

UK designation date: 6 August 2026 · Swiss internal review date: to be set internally

Relevant if: a client, UBO, payment, portfolio holding, bank, custodian or transaction has a UK nexus, or a service provider contractually applies the UK list. The UK update does not itself amend Swiss sanctions law.
