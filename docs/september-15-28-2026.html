Action required
Switzerland · 18 Sep

Revised AMLA brings sanctions risk and specified advisory activities into the implementation programme

What changed: from 1 October, prevention of breaches of Swiss Embargo Act measures becomes an express AML objective. Certain professional participation in high-risk transactions outside ordinary portfolio management also enters the adviser perimeter. Ongoing covered activity requires due diligence from 1 October and notification to the supervisory organisation or FINMA by 1 December.

Effective 1 October 2026 · existing covered advisory activity: notify by 1 December 2026

Action: update the AML risk analysis, directives and sanctions controls; inventory services outside portfolio management; apply the statutory scope and exemption test to each ongoing mandate; and assign execution, control and director approval. Given the IAM's Russian-client and cross-border profile, any low sanctions-exposure conclusion requires specific documented support.

Action required
Switzerland · 18 Sep

Transparency Register: prepare the IAM's own beneficial-owner filing

What changed: a Swiss AG must identify, verify and retain information on its beneficial owners and submit the required data through the federal register. A first Commercial Register change after 1 October triggers filing within one month; otherwise the applicable transition date depends on the legal form and audit category.

Effective 1 October 2026 · AG backstop: 1 January or 1 March 2027, depending on audit category

Action: map direct and indirect ownership and control, verify the relevant natural persons, confirm the audit category and earliest trigger, prepare AGOV/EasyGov access and designate the filing function. The governing body retains responsibility even if preparation or filing is delegated.

Action required
SECO · 18 Sep

Switzerland expands Belarus sanctions, including crypto and service restrictions

What changed: effective 19 September, Switzerland expanded trade, crypto-asset and services restrictions concerning Belarus. The package covers specified crypto-assets and central-bank digital currencies, transactions with Belarus-established crypto-service providers and decentralised platforms, managed security services and tourism services.

In force since 19 September 2026 · internal exposure review: to be set internally

Action: confirm list and rules-engine updates; re-screen clients, UBOs, wallets, counterparties and payment chains; identify direct and indirect portfolio exposure; and document the affected relationships or a supported no-exposure conclusion.

Action required
Federal Council · 25 Sep

Switzerland harmonises financial-sanctions rules in four UN regimes

What changed: amendments to the Taliban, ISIL/Al-Qaeda, DPRK and Iran ordinances harmonise immediate SECO reporting, treatment of interest and third-party credits to blocked accounts, and annual reporting of 31 December balances by 15 February for institutions that reported frozen assets.

Effective 1 November 2026 · annual balance report where applicable: 15 February

Action: update sanctions procedures and reporting templates, confirm the handling of credits and interest on blocked accounts, and configure the annual-balance control for relationships in which frozen assets have been reported.

Potentially relevant

These items do not create a generally applicable new Swiss IAM obligation. They require attention only where the stated product, exposure, counterparty practice or foreign-law nexus exists.

Potentially relevant
EU · 22–24 Sep

EU renews Russia-related listings and adds a hybrid-threat designation

What changed: the EU renewed the territorial-integrity listings for three years, until 22 September 2029, and separately listed Xenia Fedorova under the hybrid-threat regime. The review also did not renew three individuals and one entity and removed three deceased persons.

EU measures · no automatic application as Swiss law

Relevant if: an EU client, custodian, bank, venue, contract or other EU nexus is present, or a counterparty applies the EU lists contractually. A delisting under one EU regime does not by itself establish that an asset or transaction is unrestricted under every applicable regime.

Potentially relevant
OFAC · 18 Sep

OFAC extends the Lukoil International negotiation and wind-down licence

What changed: General License 131J permits specified negotiations, due diligence, contingent contracts and maintenance or wind-down involving Lukoil International GmbH and its majority-owned subsidiaries until 12:01 a.m. EDT on 22 October 2026. It does not authorise completion of a sale or general unblocking.

Licence expires 22 October 2026 · US law / counterparty-policy nexus required

Relevant if: the IAM has affected securities, funds or payment exposure, a US person or US-dollar execution chain is involved, or a custodian applies OFAC restrictions contractually.

Potentially relevant
Lenz & Staehelin · 17 Sep

Private assets: operational and conduct risks in illiquid and foreign funds

What changed: a new legal paper analyses existing Swiss duties rather than creating a new rule. It addresses competence and organisation before offering private assets, capital-call liquidity, nominee arrangements, transfer restrictions, valuation limits and conflicts, and qualified-investor status for foreign private funds.

No new external deadline · internal review: to be set internally

Relevant because: the IAM uses illiquid assets and foreign funds. The paper provides a useful test for product files, mandate terms, liquidity buffers, valuation disclosures, suitability and termination planning.
