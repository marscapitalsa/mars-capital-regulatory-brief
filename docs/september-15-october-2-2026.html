Action required
Switzerland · 1 Oct

Revised AMLA brings sanctions risk and specified advisory activities into the implementation programme

What changed: since 1 October, prevention of breaches of Swiss Embargo Act measures is an express AML objective. Certain professional participation in high-risk transactions outside ordinary portfolio management also enters the adviser perimeter. Existing covered advisory activity must be notified to the supervisory organisation or FINMA by 1 December.

In force since 1 October 2026 · existing covered advisory activity: notify by 1 December 2026

Action: update the AML risk analysis, directives and sanctions controls; inventory services outside portfolio management; apply the statutory scope and exemption test to each ongoing mandate; and assign execution, control and director approval. The open ASG / Grant Thornton article published on 28 September adds a practical implementation checklist.

Action required
Switzerland · 1 Oct

Transparency Register: prepare the IAM's own beneficial-owner filing

What changed: the register regime entered into force on 1 October. A Swiss AG must identify, verify and retain information on its beneficial owners and submit the required data. A first Commercial Register change after 1 October triggers filing within one month; otherwise the transition date depends on the audit category.

In force since 1 October 2026 · AG backstop: 1 January or 1 March 2027, depending on audit category

Action: map direct and indirect ownership and control, verify the relevant natural persons, confirm the audit category and earliest trigger, prepare AGOV/EasyGov access and designate the filing function. Keep the IAM's own filing separate from later client-side register consultation and discrepancy reporting.

Action before launch
ASG / Leonteq · 28 Sep

Planned AMC: convert flexibility into documented product governance before launch

What changed: a new ASG partner article explains the market's shift from payoff design to issuer infrastructure, lifecycle controls and role clarity. It is industry analysis, not a new rule. For the planned AMC, it reinforces existing FINMA expectations on approval, liquidity, independent pricing, conflicts, suitability and monitoring.

Trigger: before issuer appointment, product approval and first client use · no new external deadline

Action: document the issuer, manufacturer, AMC manager and distributor roles; target investors; strategy and rebalancing authority; underlying-asset eligibility; liquidity and valuation; issuer risk; conflicts and remuneration; disclosure; execution and lifecycle monitoring. The approving function and Directors should receive a complete product file before launch.

Action required
SECO · 18 Sep

Switzerland expands Belarus sanctions, including crypto and service restrictions

What changed: effective 19 September, Switzerland expanded trade, crypto-asset and services restrictions concerning Belarus. The package covers specified crypto-assets and central-bank digital currencies, transactions with Belarus-established crypto-service providers and decentralised platforms, managed security services and tourism services.

In force since 19 September 2026 · internal exposure review: to be set internally

Action: confirm list and rules-engine updates; re-screen clients, UBOs, wallets, counterparties and payment chains; identify direct and indirect portfolio exposure; and document the affected relationships or a supported no-exposure conclusion.

Action required
Federal Council · 25 Sep

Switzerland harmonises financial-sanctions rules in four UN regimes

What changed: amendments to the Taliban, ISIL/Al-Qaeda, DPRK and Iran ordinances harmonise immediate SECO reporting, treatment of interest and third-party credits to blocked accounts, and annual reporting of 31 December balances by 15 February for institutions that reported frozen assets.

Effective 1 November 2026 · annual balance report where applicable: 15 February

Action: update sanctions procedures and reporting templates, confirm the handling of credits and interest on blocked accounts, and configure the annual-balance control for relationships in which frozen assets have been reported.

Action required
FINMA / SECO · 29 Sep

Swiss ISIL/Al-Qaeda list changed: implement, screen and escalate any match

What changed: after a 28 September UN committee decision, SECO changed the Swiss sanctions database on 29 September. FINMA expressly reminded financial intermediaries to implement the prohibitions, freeze affected assets and report affected relationships to SECO; AMLA clarification and MROS duties remain separate.

Directly applicable from the UN-list change · match response: without delay

Action: obtain evidence that the screening provider loaded the 29 September change, re-screen the relevant population, resolve possible matches and follow the SECO plus AMLA/MROS escalation chain where a match or suspicion exists.

Priority 2

Potentially relevant

These items do not create a generally applicable new Swiss IAM obligation. They require attention only where the stated client, product, transmission, exposure, counterparty practice or foreign-law nexus exists.

Potentially relevant
FINMA · 29 Sep

Russian PEP enforcement shows where risk appetite and KYC exceptions can fail

What changed: FINMA concluded a bank-specific enforcement case involving private-debt risk and clients linked to two Russian PEPs. It found inadequate source-of-wealth scrutiny, weak treatment of negative media and suspicious conduct, an unjustified KYC policy exception, conflicts involving an employee and external intermediaries, and AMLA reporting breaches.

Bank-specific decision; not a new general rule · IAM review: to be set internally

Relevant because: the IAM has Russian clients and an introducer relationship, although it has stated that it has no PEP clients. Confirm that this remains true, that PEP links and negative media are escalated, that any exception is independently approved and periodically challenged, and that introducer remuneration cannot override the AML risk assessment.

Potentially relevant
FINMA · 30 Sep

FINMA consults on revised rules for direct transmission to foreign authorities

What changed: FINMA opened consultation on a partial revision of Circular 2017/6 following Parliament's amendment of Article 42c FINMASA. The draft distinguishes supervisory-purpose transmissions from other transmissions and reflects a legal presumption concerning confidentiality and purpose limitation for certain supervisory transfers.

Consultation closes 27 November 2026 · draft, not final supervisory practice

Relevant if: the IAM sends non-public information directly to a foreign supervisory authority or relies on Article 42c for cross-border disclosure. Otherwise, record the item as monitored without an implementation project.

Potentially relevant
EU · 22–24 Sep

EU renews Russia-related listings and adds a hybrid-threat designation

What changed: the EU renewed the territorial-integrity listings for three years, until 22 September 2029, and separately listed Xenia Fedorova under the hybrid-threat regime. The review also did not renew three individuals and one entity and removed three deceased persons.

EU measures · no automatic application as Swiss law

Relevant if: an EU client, custodian, bank, venue, contract or other EU nexus is present, or a counterparty applies the EU lists contractually. A delisting under one EU regime does not by itself establish that an asset or transaction is unrestricted under every applicable regime.

EU · 28 Sep

EU lists 10 people and 17 entities over deportation of Ukrainian children

What changed: the EU added 10 individuals and 17 entities to its territorial-integrity regime for responsibility connected with unlawful deportation, forced transfer and assimilation of Ukrainian children. The measures include asset freezes and a prohibition on making funds or economic resources available.

EU measure from 28 September 2026 · no automatic application as Swiss law

Relevant if: the IAM has an EU nexus or a bank, custodian, broker, fund or contractual framework applies EU lists. Confirm the latest list version before making a match or release decision.

EU lists 10 Russian officials and judges under its Russia human-rights regime

What changed: the EU listed 10 individuals connected with decisions and proceedings that barred Yabloko from the State Duma elections and sentenced opposition figures. Listed persons are subject to an EU asset freeze and non-availability prohibition.

EU measure from 28 September 2026 · no automatic application as Swiss law

Relevant if: the IAM has an EU or contractual nexus. This is a separate EU human-rights regime and should not be conflated with the territorial-integrity listings or Swiss sanctions.

UK · 1–2 Oct

UK adds 23 targets and eight ships, then remakes two ship specifications

What changed: on 1 October, the UK added 23 individuals and entities and eight ships under its Russia regime. On 2 October, it revoked and remade the specifications for GALLE ENERGY and BEBEK-E under new entries and made two administrative corrections; the ships remained restricted.

UK measures from 1–2 October 2026 · no automatic application as Swiss law

Relevant if: a UK person, branch, service, custodian, broker, currency or contract supplies the nexus, or a provider contractually applies the UK list. Screening evidence should use the 2 October list state rather than only the original 1 October package.

OFAC · 18 Sep

OFAC extends the Lukoil International negotiation and wind-down licence

What changed: General License 131J permits specified negotiations, due diligence, contingent contracts and maintenance or wind-down involving Lukoil International GmbH and its majority-owned subsidiaries until 12:01 a.m. EDT on 22 October 2026. It does not authorise completion of a sale or general unblocking.

Licence expires 22 October 2026 · US law / counterparty-policy nexus required

Relevant if: the IAM has affected securities, funds or payment exposure, a US person or US-dollar execution chain is involved, or a custodian applies OFAC restrictions contractually.

Lenz & Staehelin · 17 Sep

Private assets: operational and conduct risks in illiquid and foreign funds

What changed: a new legal paper analyses existing Swiss duties rather than creating a new rule. It addresses competence and organisation before offering private assets, capital-call liquidity, nominee arrangements, transfer restrictions, valuation limits and conflicts, and qualified-investor status for foreign private funds.

No new external deadline · internal review: to be set internally

Relevant because: the IAM uses illiquid assets and foreign funds. The paper provides a useful test for product files, mandate terms, liquidity buffers, valuation disclosures, suitability and termination planning.
