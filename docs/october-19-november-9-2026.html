Action required
Switzerland · 4 Nov · FINMA reminder 6 Nov

Switzerland adds 254 asset-freeze listings

What changed: Switzerland added 254 individuals and entities to asset-freeze and non-availability measures and removed six individuals and one company. FINMA reminded financial intermediaries that reporting to SECO does not replace the separate AMLA assessment.

Effective 4 November 2026, 23:00 Swiss time · report without delay where triggered

Action: Refresh Swiss screening. For a confirmed exposure, assess the freeze, SECO reporting and the two-week transaction lookback; assess AMLA separately.

Action required
Switzerland · 4–5 Nov

Withdrawals from newly listed banks and crypto firms

What changed: Swiss transaction bans now cover 33 Russian banks, 17 financial and crypto firms and Eco-Islamic Bank. The ban can cover withdrawals of a client’s own funds and instructions by a Swiss IAM. The same institutions are listed by the EU, whose rules apply separately where the person or transaction falls within EU scope.

SECO applications: three months from the Swiss listing · where applicable, EU applications: 13 or 23 November 2026

Action: If exposed, confirm the exact entity and all banks in the payment chain. Do not instruct a prohibited leg without a valid exception or permission.

Potentially relevant
FATF · 30 Oct

FATF removes four jurisdictions from its grey list

What changed: FATF removed Bulgaria, Côte d’Ivoire, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Monaco from increased monitoring. Eighteen jurisdictions remain; the composition of the high-risk list is unchanged.

Published 30 October 2026 · internal review date: To be set internally

Why it matters: If one of the four jurisdictions affects a client, beneficial owner or regular payment corridor, or the IAM’s country-risk matrix uses FATF status, review that risk factor. Do not automatically downgrade the relationship or stop enhanced due diligence (EDD): other risk factors or requirements may still apply.

Potentially relevant
Switzerland · 5 Nov

Switzerland widens the crypto-provider ownership restriction

What changed: The amended Swiss rule covers acquisition of Swiss- or EEA-established or constituted entities providing crypto-asset services, regardless of the type of crypto service. Article 20(2bis) also covers control and management roles; paragraph 3 sets out exceptions.

Effective 5 November 2026 · assess before an affected acquisition, control change or appointment

Relevant if: a mandate, vehicle or deal involves a covered acquirer, controller or appointee and a crypto-service business. This is not a general ban on owning Bitcoin.

Potentially relevant
FINMA · 20 Oct

FINMA approves Switzerland’s first spot Bitcoin ETF

What changed: FINMA approved the first Swiss exchange-traded retail fund investing directly in Bitcoin, with a fund-specific diversification exemption. FINMA stressed that the wrapper does not remove Bitcoin volatility or the risk of substantial losses.

Approval announced 20 October 2026 · no new general deadline

Relevant if: offering, recommending or allocating to a crypto-linked instrument: review risk disclosure, suitability where applicable and the proposed position size.

OFAC / US DOJ · 27 Oct

Extreme Networks: screening the name on the paperwork was not enough

What changed: OFAC announced a $1.53 million settlement over nine apparent violations involving networking equipment and services for a Russian defence manufacturer blocked through majority ownership. The US DOJ separately resolved its investigation through a non-prosecution agreement.

Enforcement case · no new Swiss legal deadline

Relevant if: Relevant where a client, introducer or payment intermediary supplies conflicting information about the true beneficiary, end user or ownership chain.

OFAC · 26 Oct

OFAC updates its guidance on blocking, rejecting and reporting

What changed: OFAC refreshed 11 FAQs on when to block rather than reject, how to handle blocked accounts and how to report. The update explains the existing reporting framework; it is not a new Swiss filing rule.

US reports: 10 business days after the applicable blocking or rejection, where required

Relevant if: an affected payment uses a US bank or the IAM has its own US reporting nexus. Confirm what the institution did and who must report.

OFAC · 21 Oct

Lukoil International: OFAC extends limited sale and wind-down permissions

What changed: General License 131K replaced 131J. It permits specified contingent sale negotiations and necessary maintenance or wind-down for Lukoil International and its covered entities; it does not authorise completion of the sale or payments to Russia.

Through 12:01 a.m. US Eastern Standard Time on 21 November 2026

Relevant if: there is covered Lukoil International exposure and a US legal or provider-policy nexus. Check the exact activity and licence version.

OFAC lists Russia-linked facilitators of North Korean IT-worker fraud
